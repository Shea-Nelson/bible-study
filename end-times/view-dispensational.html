View

Dispensational premillennialism (pretribulation)

Christ comes for His church before a seven-year tribulation (the rapture), then returns with His church after it to judge the beast and set up a literal thousand-year kingdom on earth, centered in Jerusalem, where God's unconditional promises to national Israel are fulfilled. After a final revolt, the great white throne judgment and the eternal state follow. The key commitments are a consistently literal reading of prophecy and a lasting distinction between Israel and the church.

Order of events

  1. Church age (the "mystery" parenthesis between Daniel's 69th and 70th weeks)
  2. Pretribulation rapture: the dead in Christ rise and living believers are caught up (1 Thess 4:16-17)
  3. Judgment seat of Christ and marriage of the Lamb in heaven
  4. The Antichrist confirms a seven-year covenant with Israel (Dan 9:27)
  5. First 3½ years: seals, the 144,000 sealed, the two witnesses
  6. Midpoint: abomination of desolation in a rebuilt temple; the Antichrist claims deity
  7. Great tribulation (3½ years): trumpets, bowls, the mark of the beast
  8. Armageddon; Christ returns with His saints (Rev 19)
  9. Israel's national repentance (Zech 12:10); judgment of the nations (Matt 25)
  10. Satan bound; tribulation saints raised; the 1,000-year reign from Jerusalem
  11. Satan released; Gog and Magog revolt crushed (Rev 20:7-10)
  12. Great white throne judgment of the unbelieving dead
  13. New heavens and new earth; the New Jerusalem

Who holds it

  • John Nelson Darby (1800-1882) — Plymouth Brethren founder and systematizer of dispensationalism and the pretribulation rapture
  • C. I. Scofield — The Scofield Reference Bible (1909; rev. 1917)
  • Lewis Sperry Chafer — Systematic Theology (8 vols., 1947-48); founder of Dallas Theological Seminary
  • John F. Walvoord — The Rapture Question (1957); The Millennial Kingdom (1959); The Revelation of Jesus Christ (1966); Daniel: The Key to Prophetic Revelation (1971)
  • J. Dwight Pentecost — Things to Come (1958)
  • Charles C. Ryrie — Dispensationalism Today (1965), revised as Dispensationalism (1995; 2007): the three "sine qua non"
  • Paul D. Feinberg — the pretribulation case in Reiter, ed., Three Views on the Rapture (1984)
  • Arnold Fruchtenbaum — The Footsteps of the Messiah (1982; rev. 2003)
  • John MacArthur — The Second Coming (1999); MacArthur New Testament Commentary: Revelation 1-11 (1999) and 12-22 (2000); the 2007 Shepherds' Conference message "Why Every Self-Respecting Calvinist Is a Premillennialist"
  • Craig Blaising and Darrell Bock — Progressive Dispensationalism (1993), a revised form
  • Popular works — Hal Lindsey, The Late Great Planet Earth (1970); Tim LaHaye and Jerry B. Jenkins, the Left Behind novels (1995-2007)

Strengths

  • Takes Old Testament land and kingdom promises to Israel at face value (Rom 11:29, "the gifts and the calling of God are irrevocable").
  • Gives Daniel 9:27 a coherent future setting.
  • Reads Revelation 19-20 in sequence: return, then binding, then a thousand years.
  • Strong emphasis on readiness and imminence.

Hard questions

  • No text clearly separates a rapture from the visible coming by seven years. Matt 24:29-31 and 2 Thess 2:1-3 place the gathering "after the tribulation."
  • The 2,000-year gap between Daniel's 69th and 70th weeks is not stated in the text.
  • The system is historically recent (1830s onward), though its defenders note earlier hints.
  • Would Gentile believers be second-class or separate from Israel's promises (Eph 2:14-16; Gal 3:29)? Progressive dispensationalism tries to answer this.

Solomon's assessment

Dispensationalism has given the church zeal for prophecy, love for Israel, and serious attention to the text. I am not persuaded by the pretribulation rapture. The New Testament presents one visible coming that gathers the saints after the tribulation (Matt 24:29-31; 2 Thess 2:1-8). But this is a family disagreement among Bible-believing Christians, not a test of orthodoxy.

Common ground

Whatever the view: Christ returns personally and visibly, the dead are raised bodily, all are judged, and God makes all things new (Apostles' and Nicene Creeds).
