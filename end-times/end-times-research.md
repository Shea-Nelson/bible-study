# What the Bible Says About the End Times

*Research for Shea's AI-era novel. Prepared by Solomon. Scripture quotations are from the LSB; read full passages at https://lsbible.org.*

## The shared core

- Christ will return personally, bodily, and visibly (Acts 1:11; Apostles' and Nicene Creeds).
- The dead will be raised bodily (John 5:28-29; 1 Cor 15).
- There will be a final judgment by Christ (Matt 25:31-46; Rev 20:11-15).
- God will make a new heavens and new earth (Isa 65:17; 2 Pet 3:13; Rev 21-22).
- No one knows the day or hour (Matt 24:36).

## Glossary (100 terms)

### 144,000  [debated]

*Greek hekaton tesserakonta tessares chiliades*

Those sealed "from every tribe of the sons of Israel" (Rev 7:4-8), 12,000 from each of twelve tribes, later seen with the Lamb on Mount Zion (14:1-5). Dispensationalists see Jewish believers (often evangelists) during the tribulation. Many others (Beale, Bauckham) see the whole people of God as an army counted by tribes, the same group John then sees as "a great multitude which no one could count" (7:9). John hears a number, then sees a multitude, just as he hears of a Lion and sees a Lamb in 5:5-6.

Key verses: Revelation 7:1-17; Revelation 14:1-5

### 666 (number of the beast)  [debated]

*Greek hexakosioi hexēkonta hex*

"Let him who has understanding calculate the number of the beast, for it is the number of man; and his number is 666" (Rev 13:18). In gematria, Hebrew letters for "Nero Caesar" (NRWN QSR) add up to 666, and the variant reading 616 matches the Latin spelling. Many scholars (for example, Richard Bauckham) find this persuasive for the first-century reference. Irenaeus (Against Heresies 5.30) knew the 616 variant, offered names like Lateinos and Teitan, and warned against being certain. Others read 666 symbolically, as falling short of 7 (perfection) at every level.

Key verses: Revelation 13:18; Revelation 15:2

### 70 weeks (seventy sevens) of Daniel  [debated]

*Hebrew shabuʿim shibʿim (שָׁבֻעִים שִׁבְעִים)* · Strong's: H7620 (Daniel 9:24), H7657 (Daniel 9:24)

Gabriel's prophecy that "seventy weeks" (seventy sevens, usually understood as 490 years) are decreed "to finish the transgression, to make an end of sin, to make atonement for iniquity, to bring in everlasting righteousness" (Dan 9:24-27). The 69 weeks lead "until Messiah the Prince." Interpretations of the 70th week: (1) Dispensationalists put a gap between week 69 and week 70 and read week 70 as a future seven-year tribulation, with "he" in 9:27 as the Antichrist making and breaking a covenant (Sir Robert Anderson, The Coming Prince, 1881; Harold Hoehner, Chronological Aspects of the Life of Christ, 1977). (2) Many Reformed and early Christian readers see week 70 fulfilled in Christ, who confirmed the covenant, ended sacrifice by His death in the middle of the week, and brought the desolation of AD 70 (E. J. Young, The Prophecy of Daniel, 1949; Meredith Kline, "The Covenant of the Seventieth Week," 1974). (3) Critical scholars apply it to Antiochus IV.

Key verses: Daniel 9:24-27

### Abaddon / Apollyon  [debated]

*Hebrew ʾabaddon (אֲבַדּוֹן), "destruction"; Greek Apollyōn (Ἀπολλύων), "destroyer"* · Strong's: G3 (Revelation 9:11), G623 (Revelation 9:11), H11 (Job 26:6)

"The angel of the abyss," king over the locusts (Rev 9:11). Abaddon is also an Old Testament name for the realm of destruction (Job 26:6; Prov 15:11). Some identify him with Satan, others with a lesser demon, and a few, unpersuasively, with Christ.

Key verses: Revelation 9:11; Job 26:6; Proverbs 15:11

### Abomination of desolation  [debated]

*Greek to bdelygma tēs erēmōseōs (τὸ βδέλυγμα τῆς ἐρημώσεως); Hebrew shiqquts meshomem* · Strong's: G946 (Matthew 24:15), G2050 (Matthew 24:15), H8251 (Daniel 9:27), H8074 (Daniel 9:27)

A sacrilege that defiles the holy place and brings desolation (Dan 9:27; 11:31; 12:11). The first fulfillment came when Antiochus IV Epiphanes set up a pagan altar in the temple in 167 BC (1 Maccabees 1:54 uses the phrase). Jesus pointed to a further fulfillment (Matt 24:15). Luke's parallel says "when you see Jerusalem surrounded by armies" (Luke 21:20), and preterists tie it to AD 70. Futurists also expect a final fulfillment by the Antichrist in a rebuilt temple (2 Thess 2:4).

Key verses: Daniel 9:27; Daniel 11:31; Daniel 12:11; Matthew 24:15; Luke 21:20; 2 Thessalonians 2:4

### Abyss (bottomless pit)  [shared]

*Greek abyssos (ἄβυσσος)* · Strong's: G12 (Revelation 9:1)

The prison of demonic powers (Luke 8:31). The locusts rise from it (Rev 9:1-11), the beast ascends from it (11:7; 17:8), and Satan is locked in it for the thousand years (20:1-3).

Key verses: Luke 8:31; Revelation 9:1-11; Revelation 11:7; Revelation 20:1-3

### Already / not yet (inaugurated eschatology)  [shared]

*Theological term*

The view that the end has already begun in Christ (the resurrection is the "first fruits," 1 Cor 15:20) but is not yet complete. Geerhardus Vos and George Eldon Ladd developed it in different ways, and Oscar Cullmann pictured it as D-Day (the decisive battle) before V-Day (the final victory) in Christ and Time (1946). Most evangelical scholars across the millennial views use this framework today.

Key verses: 1 Corinthians 15:20-23; Matthew 12:28; Romans 8:23

### Amillennialism  [debated]

*"No (literal) millennium"*

The view that the thousand years symbolize the present church age, from Christ's first coming to His return. During it Satan is bound so that he cannot deceive the nations or stop the gospel, and departed saints reign with Christ in heaven. Christ returns once, and the general resurrection, the judgment, and the new creation follow at once. Augustine (City of God 20.7-9) is its classic source, and modern defenders include Anthony Hoekema (The Bible and the Future, 1979), who suggested "realized millennialism" as a better name, Kim Riddlebarger (A Case for Amillennialism, 2003), Sam Storms (Kingdom Come, 2013), and G. K. Beale.

Key verses: Revelation 20:1-6; Matthew 12:28-29; John 5:28-29; John 12:31-32

### Ancient of Days  [shared]

*Aramaic ʿattiq yomin (עַתִּיק יוֹמִין)* · Strong's: H6268 (Daniel 7:9), H3118 (Daniel 7:9)

Daniel's title for God enthroned in judgment, with white garments, hair like pure wool, and a throne of fire (Dan 7:9-10). The "Son of Man" comes to Him and receives an everlasting kingdom (7:13-14). In Revelation 1:14 the risen Christ shares the same white-haired imagery.

Key verses: Daniel 7:9-14; Daniel 7:22; Revelation 1:13-14

### Antichrist  [debated]

*Greek antichristos (ἀντίχριστος), "against / in place of Christ"* · Strong's: G500 (1 John 2:18)

Used only in John's letters (1 John 2:18, 22; 4:3; 2 John 7). John says "antichrist is coming" and that "even now many antichrists have appeared" — anyone who denies the Father and the Son. The church has commonly linked this figure with Paul's man of lawlessness and Revelation's beast, though whether these are one person is debated.

Key verses: 1 John 2:18-22; 1 John 4:3; 2 John 7

### Apocalyptic (genre)  [shared]

*Literary category*

A kind of writing (Daniel 7-12, Ezekiel 38-39, Zechariah, Revelation) that uses visions, numbers, beasts, and cosmic imagery to unveil heaven's view of history. Readers agree the genre is highly symbolic. They disagree about how far each symbol points to a literal future person or event.

Key verses: Daniel 7:1-3; Revelation 1:1

### Apokalypsis (revelation)  [shared]

*Greek apokalypsis (ἀποκάλυψις), "unveiling"* · Strong's: G602 (Revelation 1:1)

An "unveiling." It gives the last book of the Bible its name (Rev 1:1) and also describes Christ's being revealed at His return (1 Pet 1:7; 2 Thess 1:7).

Key verses: Revelation 1:1; 2 Thessalonians 1:7; 1 Peter 1:7

### Archangel  [shared]

*Greek archangelos (ἀρχάγγελος)* · Strong's: G743 (1 Thessalonians 4:16)

"Chief angel." Michael is the only one named as such (Jude 9). The archangel's voice sounds when Christ descends (1 Thess 4:16).

Key verses: 1 Thessalonians 4:16; Jude 9; Daniel 10:13; Daniel 12:1

### Armageddon  [debated]

*Greek Harmagedōn (Ἁρμαγεδών); usually explained as Hebrew har Megiddo, "mountain of Megiddo"* · Strong's: G717 (Revelation 16:16)

The place where demonic spirits gather "the kings of the whole world" for "the war of the great day of God, the Almighty" (Rev 16:14-16). Megiddo was a famous battlefield (Judg 5:19; 2 Kgs 23:29; Zech 12:11), but it is a plain with no mountain, so many see a symbolic name for the final confrontation. Futurists usually expect a literal gathering in the Jezreel Valley. Revelation never describes an actual battle: the enemy is destroyed by the word from Christ's mouth (19:15, 21).

Key verses: Revelation 16:12-16; Revelation 19:11-21; Zechariah 12:11; Zechariah 14:1-4

### Babylon the Great  [debated]

*Greek Babylōn hē megalē (Βαβυλὼν ἡ μεγάλη)* · Strong's: G897 (Revelation 17:5)

The harlot city, "the mother of harlots," drunk with the blood of the saints, seated on the beast and on seven mountains. She is the hub of the world's luxury trade and falls in one hour (Rev 14:8; 17-18). Proposed identifications: imperial Rome (1 Pet 5:13 already uses "Babylon" for Rome), apostate Jerusalem (Gentry and other preterists), a rebuilt literal Babylon in Iraq (some dispensationalists), apostate religion, or the world's seductive economic and cultural system in every age (idealists).

Key verses: Revelation 14:8; Revelation 17:1-18; Revelation 18:1-24; Jeremiah 51:6-8; 1 Peter 5:13

### Beast  [shared]

*Greek thērion (θηρίον), "wild animal"* · Strong's: G2342 (Revelation 13:1)

Revelation's term for the satanic world-power and its ruler (the beast from the sea, Rev 13:1-10) and for its religious partner (the beast from the earth, 13:11-17). It draws on Daniel 7's four beasts.

Key verses: Revelation 13:1-18; Daniel 7:2-8; Revelation 17:8-14

### Binding of Satan  [debated]

*Greek deō (δέω), "to bind"* · Strong's: G1210 (Revelation 20:2)

An angel seizes the dragon and binds him for a thousand years "so that he would not deceive the nations any longer" (Rev 20:3). Amillennialists link this to Jesus' binding of the "strong man" (Matt 12:29) and the gospel going to the nations. Premillennialists see a future total removal of Satan's influence.

Key verses: Revelation 20:1-3; Matthew 12:29; Luke 10:18; John 12:31

### Blessed hope  [shared]

*Greek elpis (ἐλπίς)* · Strong's: G1680 (Titus 2:13)

Paul's name for the believer's expectation of "the appearing of the glory of our great God and Savior, Jesus Christ" (Titus 2:13). Pretribulationists often apply it to the rapture; others apply it to the one Second Coming.

Key verses: Titus 2:13

### Book of life  [shared]

*Greek to biblion tēs zōēs* · Strong's: G975 (Revelation 20:12), G2222 (Revelation 20:12)

God's register of those who belong to Him, "the book of life of the Lamb who has been slain" (Rev 13:8). It appears from Exodus 32:32 and Daniel 12:1 through Revelation 20:12, 15, and 21:27.

Key verses: Daniel 12:1; Philippians 4:3; Revelation 13:8; Revelation 20:12-15; Revelation 21:27

### Bride of the Lamb  [shared]

*Greek nymphē (νύμφη)* · Strong's: G3565 (Revelation 21:2)

The church, the redeemed people of God, prepared for union with Christ (Rev 19:7-8; 21:9; Eph 5:25-27). In 21:9-10, John is told "I will show you the bride" and is then shown the city.

Key verses: Revelation 19:7-8; Revelation 21:2; Revelation 21:9-10; Ephesians 5:25-27; 2 Corinthians 11:2

### Chiliasm  [debated]

*Greek chilias (χιλιάς), "a thousand"*

The early-church name for belief in a literal future thousand-year earthly kingdom. Papias, Justin Martyr (Dialogue with Trypho 80-81), Irenaeus (Against Heresies 5.32-36), and Tertullian held it. Justin admits that many true Christians thought otherwise. Origen, Eusebius, and later Augustine (City of God 20.7) moved the church away from it.

Key verses: Revelation 20:4-6; Isaiah 65:17-25

### Day of the Lord  [shared]

*Hebrew yom YHWH (יוֹם יְהוָה); Greek hēmera kyriou* · Strong's: H3117 (Joel 2:31), G2250 (1 Thessalonians 5:2)

The prophets' term for God's decisive intervention to judge His enemies and save His people (Amos 5:18-20; Joel 2:31; Zeph 1:14-18). It had historical fulfillments, such as the fall of Jerusalem in 586 BC, that foreshadow the final day. The New Testament ties it to Christ's return, which comes "just like a thief in the night" (1 Thess 5:2; 2 Pet 3:10).

Key verses: Joel 2:31; Amos 5:18-20; Zephaniah 1:14-18; 1 Thessalonians 5:2; 2 Peter 3:10

### Day-year principle  [debated]

*Interpretive term*

A historicist rule that one prophetic day stands for one year (drawing on Num 14:34 and Ezek 4:6). Other approaches reject it as a general rule for Daniel and Revelation.

Key verses: Numbers 14:34; Ezekiel 4:6

### Dispensational premillennialism  [debated]

*From "dispensation" (Greek oikonomia, "stewardship")*

A premillennial system, popularized by J. N. Darby and the Scofield Reference Bible (1909), that keeps a distinction between Israel and the church. It reads prophecy with a consistently literal hermeneutic and usually expects a pretribulation rapture, a seven-year tribulation (Daniel's 70th week), and a millennium in which God's promises to national Israel are fulfilled. Charles Ryrie, in Dispensationalism (1995), names these three as its "sine qua non": the Israel/church distinction, consistent literal interpretation, and God's glory as the unifying purpose of history. Other major works are J. Dwight Pentecost, Things to Come (1958), and John Walvoord, The Millennial Kingdom (1959).

Key verses: Romans 11:25-29; Daniel 9:24-27; Revelation 20:1-6

### Dragon  [shared]

*Greek drakōn (δράκων)* · Strong's: G1404 (Revelation 12:3)

"The serpent of old who is called the devil and Satan, who deceives the whole world" (Rev 12:9). He is red, with seven heads and ten horns. He hands his power and throne to the beast (13:2), is bound (20:2), and ends in the lake of fire (20:10).

Key verses: Revelation 12:3-17; Revelation 13:2; Revelation 20:2; Genesis 3:15; Isaiah 27:1

### Eclectic approach  [debated]

*Interpretive approach*

A blend: Revelation spoke directly to its first readers (preterist), describes the whole church age symbolically (idealist), and climaxes in a still-future return of Christ (futurist). G. K. Beale's NIGTC commentary (1999) is the leading example. Many current commentators work this way.

Key verses: Revelation 1:3; Revelation 1:19; Revelation 22:20

### Epiphaneia (appearing)  [shared]

*Greek epiphaneia (ἐπιφάνεια), "appearing, manifestation"* · Strong's: G2015 (Titus 2:13)

The "appearing" of Christ in glory. It is used alongside parousia (2 Thess 2:8) and stresses that His return will be seen.

Key verses: Titus 2:13; 2 Thessalonians 2:8; 2 Timothy 4:8

### Eschatology  [shared]

*Greek eschatos (ἔσχατος), "last"* · Strong's: G2078 (1 Corinthians 15:26)

The study of "last things": the return of Christ, the resurrection, the judgment, and the final state, and also how the end has already broken into history through Jesus' death, resurrection, and the gift of the Spirit.

Key verses: 1 Corinthians 15:24-26; Hebrews 1:1-2; Acts 2:16-17

### Eschaton  [shared]

*From Greek eschatos*

"The end": the final goal and climax of history, when Christ returns and God makes all things new.

Key verses: 1 Corinthians 15:24; Matthew 24:14

### False prophet  [shared]

*Greek pseudoprophētēs (ψευδοπροφήτης)* · Strong's: G5578 (Revelation 16:13)

Revelation's later name for the beast from the earth (16:13; 19:20; 20:10), the propagandist who promotes worship of the first beast with signs. Jesus also warned of many false prophets (Matt 24:11, 24).

Key verses: Revelation 13:11-17; Revelation 19:20; Matthew 24:24

### First resurrection  [debated]

*Greek anastasis hē prōtē (ἀνάστασις ἡ πρώτη)* · Strong's: G386 (Revelation 20:5), G4413 (Revelation 20:5)

"This is the first resurrection" (Rev 20:5). Premillennialists read it as the bodily raising of believers before the millennium, with the "rest of the dead" raised after it. Amillennialists and postmillennialists usually read it as spiritual: new birth (John 5:24-25) or the soul's entry into Christ's reign at death. One bodily resurrection then comes at the end (John 5:28-29).

Key verses: Revelation 20:4-6; John 5:24-29; Daniel 12:2

### Futurism  [debated]

*Interpretive approach*

The approach that most of Revelation 4-22 (and the 70th week of Daniel) is still future, describing the final years before Christ returns. Dispensationalists and historic premillennialists hold it. It was revived in the Catholic world by the Jesuit Francisco Ribera (commentary c. 1590) and spread among Protestants through J. N. Darby in the 1800s, though early fathers such as Irenaeus and Hippolytus also expected a future Antichrist.

Key verses: Revelation 4:1; Revelation 1:19

### Gehenna (hell)  [shared]

*Greek geenna (γέεννα), from Hebrew ge hinnom, "Valley of Hinnom"* · Strong's: G1067 (Matthew 10:28)

Jesus' word for final punishment, named after the valley south of Jerusalem where children had been sacrificed (Jer 7:31-32). It is the place where God can "destroy both soul and body" (Matt 10:28).

Key verses: Matthew 10:28; Matthew 25:41; Mark 9:43-48; Jeremiah 7:31-32

### Glorification  [shared]

*Greek doxazō (δοξάζω)*

The final stage of salvation, when believers receive resurrection bodies and complete likeness to Christ (Rom 8:30; 1 John 3:2).

Key verses: Romans 8:18-30; 1 John 3:2; Philippians 3:21

### Gog and Magog  [debated]

*Hebrew Gog (גּוֹג), Magog (מָגוֹג); Greek Gōg kai Magōg* · Strong's: H1463 (Ezekiel 38:2), H4031 (Ezekiel 38:2), G1136 (Revelation 20:8), G3098 (Revelation 20:8)

In Ezekiel 38-39, Gog, "of the land of Magog," leads a coalition from the far north against restored Israel and is destroyed by God. In Revelation 20:8, "Gog and Magog" stands for the nations from the four corners of the earth that Satan gathers after the thousand years. Many dispensationalists see two separate battles, one in Ezekiel before or during the tribulation and one after the millennium. Others see Revelation 20 (and 19:17-21, which also draws on Ezek 39) as John's reuse of Ezekiel for the one final battle. The old identification of "Rosh" with Russia rests on a disputed reading of Ezek 38:2 ("chief prince" in the LSB).

Key verses: Ezekiel 38:1-23; Ezekiel 39:1-20; Revelation 20:7-10; Genesis 10:2

### Great multitude  [shared]

*Greek ochlos polys (ὄχλος πολύς)* · Strong's: G3793 (Revelation 7:9)

"A great multitude which no one could count, from every nation and all tribes and peoples and tongues," clothed in white, "the ones who come out of the great tribulation" (Rev 7:9-14).

Key verses: Revelation 7:9-17; Revelation 19:1-6

### Great tribulation  [debated]

*Greek thlipsis megalē (θλῖψις μεγάλη)* · Strong's: G2347 (Matthew 24:21)

"A great tribulation, such as has not occurred since the beginning of the world until now, nor ever will" (Matt 24:21; compare Dan 12:1; Rev 7:14). Futurists place it in the last 3½ years before the return. Preterists place it at the siege of Jerusalem in AD 66-70, which Josephus describes in Jewish War books 5-6. Some see both: AD 70 as a foreshadowing of a final distress.

Key verses: Matthew 24:21; Daniel 12:1; Revelation 7:14

### Great white throne  [shared]

*Greek thronos megas leukos (θρόνος μέγας λευκός)* · Strong's: G2362 (Revelation 20:11), G3022 (Revelation 20:11)

The last judgment: the dead, great and small, stand before God's throne and are judged "according to their deeds," and anyone not found in the book of life is thrown into the lake of fire (Rev 20:11-15). All orthodox views affirm a final judgment. They disagree on whether believers appear in this scene or in an earlier one.

Key verses: Revelation 20:11-15; Daniel 7:9-10; Matthew 25:31-46

### Historic premillennialism  [debated]

*Also "covenantal" or "classic" premillennialism*

A premillennial view without dispensationalism's sharp Israel/church divide or pretribulation rapture. The church goes through the tribulation, and Christ returns once, after it, to raise the saints and reign. Its chief modern advocate is George Eldon Ladd (The Blessed Hope, 1956; "Historic Premillennialism" in Clouse, ed., The Meaning of the Millennium, 1977). Proponents claim continuity with Justin and Irenaeus.

Key verses: Revelation 20:4-6; Matthew 24:29-31; 1 Corinthians 15:23-24

### Historicism  [debated]

*Interpretive approach*

The view that Revelation maps church history from the apostles to the end, often with a day standing for a year (so 1,260 days becomes 1,260 years). It dominated Protestant reading from the Reformation to the 1800s. Many Reformers and the Westminster Confession (25.6, 1646 text) identified the papacy as the man of sin. Representatives include Isaac Newton (Observations upon the Prophecies, 1733) and E. B. Elliott (Horae Apocalypticae, 1844). Few hold it today.

Key verses: Revelation 11:2-3; Ezekiel 4:6

### Hour of testing  [debated]

*Greek peirasmos (πειρασμός)* · Strong's: G3986 (Revelation 3:10)

Christ promises Philadelphia, "I also will keep you from the hour of testing, which is about to come upon the whole world" (Rev 3:10). Pretribulationists read "keep from" as removal. Posttribulationists compare John 17:15 (the same verb and preposition, tēreō ek) and read it as protection.

Key verses: Revelation 3:10; John 17:15

### Idealism (spiritual view)  [debated]

*Interpretive approach*

The view that Revelation pictures timeless truths: the ongoing war between Christ and Satan, the church and the world, and the sure victory of God. It does not tie the images to particular dates. It is often paired with amillennialism. William Hendriksen's More Than Conquerors (1940) is a classic modified form.

Key verses: Revelation 12:7-11; Ephesians 6:12

### Imminence  [debated]

*Theological term*

The teaching that Christ could return at any moment, so believers must always be ready (Matt 24:42-44). Pretribulationists hold that no prophesied event must come first. Most others hold that the return is always near and always to be watched for, but that some signs, such as the apostasy and the man of lawlessness (2 Thess 2:3), come first.

Key verses: Matthew 24:36; Matthew 24:42-44; James 5:8; 2 Thessalonians 2:1-3

### Intermediate state  [shared]

*Theological term*

The condition of the dead between death and resurrection. Paul would "prefer rather to be absent from the body and to be at home with the Lord" (2 Cor 5:8) and longs "to depart and be with Christ" (Phil 1:23). The dying thief is promised that he will be with Christ in Paradise that day (Luke 23:43). Yet the departed still await the resurrection of the body. The souls under the altar (Rev 6:9-11) are a picture of it.

Key verses: 2 Corinthians 5:6-8; Philippians 1:21-23; Luke 23:43; Revelation 6:9-11

### Judgment seat of Christ (bema)  [debated]

*Greek bēma (βῆμα), "tribunal, judgment seat"* · Strong's: G968 (2 Corinthians 5:10), G968 (Romans 14:10)

"We must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ" (2 Cor 5:10) to be judged for what we have done. Dispensationalists often place a separate believers' judgment for rewards in heaven after the rapture. Others see one general judgment at Christ's return in which believers are openly acquitted and rewarded.

Key verses: 2 Corinthians 5:10; Romans 14:10-12; 1 Corinthians 3:12-15

### Kingdom of God  [shared]

*Greek basileia (βασιλεία)* · Strong's: G932 (Matthew 24:14)

God's saving reign through His Messiah. It has already arrived in Jesus' ministry (Matt 12:28) and will be fully revealed at His return (Matt 25:34). All the millennial views are, at heart, arguments about how and when this kingdom shows itself on earth.

Key verses: Matthew 12:28; Matthew 24:14; Matthew 25:34; Daniel 7:14; Revelation 11:15

### Kings from the east  [debated]

*Greek basileis apo anatolēs hēliou*

The Euphrates is dried up "so that the way would be prepared for the kings from the east" (Rev 16:12). The image recalls Cyrus, who diverted the Euphrates to take Babylon, and Rome's fear of the Parthians. Some popular writers identify them with China, but the text does not.

Key verses: Revelation 16:12; Isaiah 44:27-45:1

### Lake of fire  [shared]

*Greek hē limnē tou pyros (ἡ λίμνη τοῦ πυρός)* · Strong's: G3041 (Revelation 20:10), G4442 (Revelation 20:10)

The final destiny of the beast and false prophet (19:20), the devil (20:10), Death and Hades (20:14), and all not found in the book of life (20:15). Revelation calls it "the second death" (21:8). The historic majority view is conscious, everlasting punishment (Matt 25:46). Some evangelicals (John Stott, Edward Fudge) have argued for annihilationism.

Key verses: Revelation 19:20; Revelation 20:10-15; Revelation 21:8; Matthew 25:41; Matthew 25:46

### Last days  [shared]

*Greek eschatai hēmerai* · Strong's: G2078 (Acts 2:17), G2250 (Acts 2:17)

In the New Testament, this phrase usually means the whole period that began with Christ's first coming and Pentecost and runs until His return (Acts 2:17; Heb 1:2). It is not only the final few years before the end. Some texts stress the hard times within that period (2 Tim 3:1).

Key verses: Acts 2:16-17; Hebrews 1:1-2; 2 Timothy 3:1; 1 John 2:18

### Little horn  [debated]

*Aramaic qeren zeʿerah (Dan 7:8); Hebrew qeren (Dan 8:9)* · Strong's: H7162 (Daniel 7:8)

Daniel 7: a horn that rises among the ten horns of the fourth beast, with "a mouth speaking great boasts" which wars against the saints until the Ancient of Days judges. Daniel 8: a little horn from the goat's kingdom, almost universally identified with Antiochus IV (175-164 BC). Many see the Daniel 7 horn as the final Antichrist, with Antiochus as a type of him.

Key verses: Daniel 7:7-8; Daniel 7:19-27; Daniel 8:9-14; Daniel 8:23-25

### Living creatures  [shared]

*Greek zōon (ζῷον)* · Strong's: G2226 (Revelation 4:6)

Four throne-guardians, "full of eyes," like a lion, an ox, a man, and an eagle, each with six wings, who never stop saying "Holy, holy, holy" (Rev 4:6-8). They combine Ezekiel's cherubim (Ezek 1; 10) and Isaiah's seraphim (Isa 6).

Key verses: Revelation 4:6-8; Ezekiel 1:5-14; Isaiah 6:1-3

### Man of lawlessness (man of sin)  [debated]

*Greek ho anthrōpos tēs anomias (ὁ ἄνθρωπος τῆς ἀνομίας)* · Strong's: G444 (2 Thessalonians 2:3), G458 (2 Thessalonians 2:3)

Paul's figure who will be revealed after "the apostasy". He "takes his seat in the sanctuary of God, exhibiting himself as being God" works counterfeit signs, and is destroyed by Christ "by the appearance of His coming" (2 Thess 2:3-10). Some manuscripts read "man of sin" (hamartia), as in the KJV. Most interpreters across the views expect a future individual. Preterists have proposed first-century figures, and historicists identified the papacy.

Key verses: 2 Thessalonians 2:1-12

### Maranatha  [shared]

*Aramaic, rendered in Greek as marana tha (μαράνα θά)* · Strong's: G3134 (1 Corinthians 16:22)

An Aramaic prayer meaning "Our Lord, come!" (or, read differently, "Our Lord has come"). It shows that the earliest church prayed for Christ's return. Compare Rev 22:20, "Come, Lord Jesus." The Didache (10.6) also uses it in prayer.

Key verses: 1 Corinthians 16:22; Revelation 22:20

### Mark of the beast  [debated]

*Greek charagma (χάραγμα), "stamp, engraved mark, brand"* · Strong's: G5480 (Revelation 13:16)

A mark on the right hand or forehead that the beast from the earth requires. Without it no one may "buy or sell" (Rev 13:16-17), and those who receive it share the beast's judgment (14:9-11). In the first century, charagma was used for imperial seals on documents and stamps on coins. The mark is the beast's counterfeit of God's seal on His servants' foreheads (7:3; 14:1) and echoes Deuteronomy 6:8 (hand and forehead). Futurists usually expect a literal mark tied to economic control. Idealists and many others see a symbol of allegiance and worship shown in thought and deed. Either way, Revelation treats it as a matter of worship, not an accident.

Key verses: Revelation 13:16-17; Revelation 14:9-11; Revelation 7:3; Revelation 14:1; Deuteronomy 6:8; Ezekiel 9:4

### Marriage supper of the Lamb  [shared]

*Greek to deipnon tou gamou tou arniou* · Strong's: G1173 (Revelation 19:9)

"Blessed are those who are invited to the marriage supper of the Lamb" (Rev 19:9). This is the great wedding feast of Christ and His bride, the church. It contrasts with "the great supper of God," where the birds eat the flesh of the defeated (19:17-18).

Key verses: Revelation 19:6-9; Matthew 22:1-14; Isaiah 25:6-8

### Midtribulation rapture  [debated]

*Timing view*

The rapture occurs at the midpoint of the seven years, before the great tribulation of the last three and a half years. It is often linked with the "last trumpet" (1 Cor 15:52) and the seventh trumpet (Rev 11:15). Gleason Archer defended it in Three Views on the Rapture (1984).

Key verses: 1 Corinthians 15:52; Revelation 11:15; Daniel 9:27

### Millennium  [debated]

*Latin mille, "thousand"; Greek chilioi (χίλιοι)* · Strong's: G5507 (Revelation 20:2)

The "thousand years" of Revelation 20:1-7, during which Satan is bound and the saints reign with Christ. The word appears six times in that passage. Whether it is a future earthly reign or the present age seen from heaven is the core millennial debate.

Key verses: Revelation 20:1-7

### Mystery of lawlessness  [shared]

*Greek to mystērion tēs anomias* · Strong's: G3466 (2 Thessalonians 2:7), G458 (2 Thessalonians 2:7)

Paul says the mystery of lawlessness "is already at work" (2 Thess 2:7). The spirit of rebellion is active now and will reach its peak in the lawless one.

Key verses: 2 Thessalonians 2:7

### New heavens and new earth  [shared]

*Hebrew shamayim chadashim (Isa 65:17); Greek kainos (καινός), "new in quality"* · Strong's: H2319 (Isaiah 65:17), G2537 (Revelation 21:1)

God's renewed creation, "in which righteousness dwells" (2 Pet 3:13), where He lives with His people, death and tears are gone, and "I am making all things new" (Rev 21:5). Christians debate whether the present world is renewed (Rom 8:19-23, "set free") or replaced (2 Pet 3:10). Most see deep continuity, as between Jesus' body before and after His resurrection. Jesus calls it "the regeneration" (palingenesia, Matt 19:28).

Key verses: Isaiah 65:17-25; Isaiah 66:22; Romans 8:19-23; 2 Peter 3:10-13; Revelation 21:1-5; Matthew 19:28

### New Jerusalem  [shared]

*Greek Ierousalēm kainē (Ἰερουσαλὴμ καινή)* · Strong's: G2419 (Revelation 21:2), G2537 (Revelation 21:2)

"The holy city, new Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven from God, made ready as a bride" (Rev 21:2). It is a perfect cube (21:16), like the temple's Holy of Holies, so the whole city is God's dwelling. The names of Israel's twelve tribes are on its gates and the names of the twelve apostles on its foundations. John sees no sanctuary in it, "for the Lord God the Almighty and the Lamb are its sanctuary" (21:22). Debated: whether the city is literal architecture, the glorified people of God ("the bride"), or both.

Key verses: Revelation 21:2; Revelation 21:9-27; Revelation 22:1-5; Hebrews 12:22; Galatians 4:26

### Olivet Discourse  [shared]

*Matthew 24-25; Mark 13; Luke 21*

Jesus' teaching on the Mount of Olives in answer to the disciples' questions about when the temple would fall and what would be the sign of His coming and of the end of the age (Matt 24:3). How much of it was fulfilled in AD 70 and how much is still future is one of the central debates in eschatology.

Key verses: Matthew 24:1-3; Mark 13:1-4; Luke 21:5-7

### Paradise  [shared]

*Greek paradeisos (παράδεισος), "garden, park"* · Strong's: G3857 (Luke 23:43)

The garden-presence of God. It is promised to the thief on the cross (Luke 23:43), and Paul was caught up to it (2 Cor 12:4). In Revelation 2:7, the tree of life is "in the Paradise of God," which returns in the New Jerusalem (22:2).

Key verses: Luke 23:43; Revelation 2:7; Revelation 22:1-2

### Parousia  [shared]

*Greek parousia (παρουσία), "presence, arrival"* · Strong's: G3952 (Matthew 24:3)

"Coming" or "arrival." It was used for a royal visit and became the standard term for Christ's return in glory. Every orthodox view affirms a personal, visible parousia.

Key verses: Matthew 24:3; Matthew 24:27; 1 Thessalonians 4:15; 2 Thessalonians 2:8; 1 Corinthians 15:23

### Postmillennialism  [debated]

*"After the millennium"*

The view that Christ returns after (post) a long golden age, produced by the Spirit-blessed spread of the gospel, in which most of the world is converted and society is deeply Christianized. It was widespread among Puritans (Iain Murray, The Puritan Hope, 1971) and Jonathan Edwards. Its defenders include Loraine Boettner (The Millennium, 1957), Kenneth Gentry (He Shall Have Dominion, 1992), Keith Mathison (Postmillennialism, 1999), and Douglas Wilson (Heaven Misplaced, 2008). It differs from amillennialism mainly in its optimism about history before the return.

Key verses: Psalm 110:1-2; Matthew 13:31-33; Matthew 28:18-20; 1 Corinthians 15:25; Isaiah 2:2-4

### Posttribulation rapture  [debated]

*Timing view*

The church goes through the tribulation, protected from God's wrath but not from persecution, and is caught up to meet the Lord as He comes, then accompanies Him to earth (like citizens going out to meet an arriving king; Greek apantēsis, 1 Thess 4:17). This view is held by historic premillennialists (Ladd, The Blessed Hope), by Robert Gundry (The Church and the Tribulation, 1973), and by Douglas Moo. Amillennialists and postmillennialists also expect one coming after the tribulation, so in practice they hold this timing too.

Key verses: Matthew 24:29-31; 2 Thessalonians 2:1-3; 1 Thessalonians 4:17; Revelation 20:4

### Pre-wrath rapture  [debated]

*Timing view*

The rapture comes after the midpoint, during the great tribulation, when the cosmic signs of the sixth seal announce "the great day of their wrath" (Rev 6:12-17), and before the trumpet and bowl judgments. It was set out by Marvin Rosenthal (The Pre-Wrath Rapture of the Church, 1990) and Robert Van Kampen (The Sign, 1992), and defended by Alan Hultberg in Three Views on the Rapture (2nd ed., 2010).

Key verses: Matthew 24:21-31; Revelation 6:12-17; Revelation 7:9-14

### Premillennialism  [debated]

*"Before the millennium"*

The view that Christ returns before (pre) the millennium and then reigns on earth for a thousand years before the final judgment and new creation. It has two main forms, historic and dispensational.

Key verses: Revelation 19:11-20:6; 1 Corinthians 15:23-25

### Preterism (full, or hyper-preterism)  [debated]

*Latin praeter, "past"*

The view that all prophecy, including the Second Coming and the resurrection, was fulfilled by AD 70. It denies a future bodily return and a future bodily resurrection. That puts it outside the Apostles' and Nicene Creeds, and Reformed critics, partial preterists included, reject it (Keith Mathison, ed., When Shall These Things Be?, 2004). Its key texts are J. Stuart Russell's The Parousia (1878) and Max King's The Spirit of Prophecy (1971).

Paul condemns teachers who say the resurrection has already taken place, which upsets the faith of some (2 Tim 2:18).

Key verses: 2 Timothy 2:17-18; 1 Corinthians 15:12-19

### Preterism (partial)  [debated]

*Latin praeter, "past"*

The view that many prophecies, especially the Olivet Discourse and much of Revelation, were fulfilled in the first century, above all in the fall of Jerusalem in AD 70. It still affirms a future bodily return of Christ, resurrection, and final judgment. Proponents include R. C. Sproul (The Last Days According to Jesus), Kenneth Gentry, and Gary DeMar. The Jesuit Luis de Alcázar wrote an early systematic preterist commentary (1614).

Key verses: Matthew 24:34; Revelation 1:1; Revelation 1:3; Revelation 22:10

### Pretribulation rapture  [debated]

*Timing view*

Christ comes for His church before a seven-year tribulation, then returns with His saints afterward. It is held by most dispensationalists: Darby, Scofield, Walvoord (The Rapture Question, 1957), Pentecost, Ryrie, MacArthur, and Paul Feinberg (in Three Views on the Rapture, 1984). Key arguments: the church is promised deliverance from wrath (1 Thess 1:10; 5:9; Rev 3:10), the word "church" does not appear in Revelation 4-19, and imminence.

Key verses: 1 Thessalonians 1:10; 1 Thessalonians 5:9; Revelation 3:10; John 14:1-3

### Progressive dispensationalism  [debated]

*Theological term*

A newer form (Craig Blaising and Darrell Bock, Progressive Dispensationalism, 1993) that sees the Davidic kingdom as already inaugurated in Christ's reign at God's right hand, with more continuity between Israel and the church, while still expecting a future earthly millennium with a restored Israel.

Key verses: Acts 2:30-36; Romans 11:26

### Prophetic perspective (telescoping)  [shared]

*Interpretive term*

Prophets often saw near and far events together, like mountain peaks that look close from a distance, without showing the valley between them. Isaiah 61:1-2, which Jesus read and stopped mid-verse in Luke 4:18-19, is a common example.

Key verses: Isaiah 61:1-2; Luke 4:18-21

### Rapture  [shared]

*Latin rapiemur (Vulgate, 1 Thess 4:17); Greek harpazō (ἁρπάζω), "to snatch, catch up"* · Strong's: G726 (1 Thessalonians 4:17)

Believers alive at Christ's coming will be "caught up together with them in the clouds to meet the Lord in the air" (1 Thess 4:17). Every view affirms this catching up. The debate is about its timing in relation to the tribulation, and whether it is a separate event from the visible Second Coming.

Key verses: 1 Thessalonians 4:13-18; 1 Corinthians 15:51-52; John 14:1-3

### Realized eschatology  [debated]

*Theological term (C. H. Dodd)*

C. H. Dodd's view, set out in The Parables of the Kingdom (1935), that the kingdom fully arrived in Jesus' ministry. Taken to the extreme, it leaves no real future coming. Orthodox teaching rejects that extreme while keeping the "already."

Key verses: Luke 17:20-21

### Recapitulation (progressive parallelism)  [debated]

*Interpretive term*

The idea that Revelation's sections (for example, the seals, trumpets, and bowls) do not run strictly in order but cover the same period from different angles, each building toward the end. Victorinus of Pettau (c. 260) and Augustine held forms of it, and Hendriksen sees seven parallel sections. Futurists usually read the judgments as consecutive.

Key verses: Revelation 6:12-17; Revelation 11:15-19; Revelation 16:17-21

### Regeneration (palingenesia)  [shared]

*Greek palingenesia (παλιγγενεσία), "rebirth"* · Strong's: G3824 (Matthew 19:28)

"In the regeneration when the Son of Man will sit on His glorious throne" (Matt 19:28). This is the cosmic rebirth of all things at the end. Compare Peter's "restoration of all things" (apokatastasis, Acts 3:21).

Key verses: Matthew 19:28; Acts 3:21

### Resurrection of the body  [shared]

*Greek anastasis (ἀνάστασις)* · Strong's: G386 (John 5:29)

The bodily raising of the dead, "those who did the good deeds to a resurrection of life, those who committed the evil deeds to a resurrection of judgment" (John 5:29; Dan 12:2; Acts 24:15). Believers will be raised imperishable, like Christ's glorious body (1 Cor 15:42-53; Phil 3:21). This is confessed in every creed.

Key verses: Daniel 12:2; John 5:28-29; Acts 24:15; 1 Corinthians 15:42-53; Philippians 3:20-21

### Satan / the devil  [shared]

*Hebrew satan (שָׂטָן), "adversary"; Greek diabolos (διάβολος), "slanderer"* · Strong's: G4567 (Revelation 20:7), G1228 (Revelation 12:9)

The adversary and accuser, the fallen angel behind the world's rebellion. Revelation gives him four names together: the dragon, the serpent of old, the devil, and Satan (12:9; 20:2).

Key verses: Job 1:6-12; Zechariah 3:1-2; Revelation 12:9-10; Revelation 20:2; 1 Peter 5:8

### Second Coming (Second Advent)  [shared]

*Hebrews 9:28, "a second time"*

Christ's future, bodily, visible, and glorious return to raise the dead, judge the world, and bring in the new creation. It is confessed in the Apostles' and Nicene Creeds (Apostles' Creed: He "will come again to judge the living and the dead"; Nicene Creed: "he will come again in glory to judge the living and the dead").

Key verses: Acts 1:11; Hebrews 9:28; Revelation 1:7; Matthew 25:31

### Second death  [shared]

*Greek ho thanatos ho deuteros (ὁ θάνατος ὁ δεύτερος)* · Strong's: G2288 (Revelation 20:14), G1208 (Revelation 20:14)

Final judgment in the lake of fire. Revelation defines it this way (20:14; 21:8). It has no authority over those who share in the first resurrection (20:6).

Key verses: Revelation 2:11; Revelation 20:6; Revelation 20:14; Revelation 21:8

### Seven bowls  [shared]

*Greek phialē (φιάλη), "shallow bowl"* · Strong's: G5357 (Revelation 16:1)

The final, complete outpouring of God's wrath ("in them the wrath of God is finished," 15:1): sores, sea and rivers turned to blood, scorching sun, darkness on the beast's throne, the Euphrates dried up for Armageddon, and the great earthquake that splits Babylon (Rev 15-16).

Key verses: Revelation 15:1-8; Revelation 16:1-21

### Seven seals  [shared]

*Greek sphragis (σφραγίς)* · Strong's: G4973 (Revelation 6:1)

The Lamb alone is worthy to open the sealed scroll (Rev 5). Its seals release the four horsemen (conquest, war, famine, death), the cry of the martyrs, cosmic upheaval and "the great day of their wrath," and then silence in heaven before the trumpets (Rev 6:1-8:1).

Key verses: Revelation 5:1-9; Revelation 6:1-17; Revelation 8:1

### Seven spirits of God  [debated]

*Greek ta hepta pneumata (τὰ ἑπτὰ πνεύματα)* · Strong's: G4151 (Revelation 1:4)

"The seven Spirits who are before His throne" (Rev 1:4; 3:1; 4:5; 5:6). Most interpreters (for example, Bauckham and Beale) read this as the Holy Spirit in His fullness, drawing on the sevenfold lamp and "seven eyes" of Zechariah 4. Some read seven chief angels.

Key verses: Revelation 1:4; Revelation 4:5; Revelation 5:6; Zechariah 4:2-10; Isaiah 11:2

### Seven trumpets  [shared]

*Greek salpinx (σάλπιγξ)* · Strong's: G4536 (Revelation 8:2)

Warning judgments that strike a third of the earth, sea, rivers, and lights. They echo the plagues of Egypt. The fifth and sixth trumpets are the demonic locusts and the 200-million horsemen, and the seventh announces that "the kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord and of His Christ" (Rev 8:2-11:19).

Key verses: Revelation 8:2-13; Revelation 9:1-21; Revelation 11:15-19

### Seventieth week  [debated]

*Hebrew shabuaʿ ʾechad, "one week" (Dan 9:27)* · Strong's: H7620 (Daniel 9:27)

The final "seven" of Daniel 9:27, split in the middle ("in the middle of the week he will make sacrifice and grain offering cease"). Revelation's 1,260 days, 42 months, and "time, times, and half a time" are often tied to its halves.

Key verses: Daniel 9:27; Revelation 11:2-3; Revelation 12:6; Revelation 12:14; Revelation 13:5

### Sheep and goats judgment  [debated]

*Matthew 25:31-46*

When the Son of Man comes in glory and sits on His throne, "all the nations" are gathered and separated as a shepherd separates sheep from goats, to "eternal punishment" or "eternal life" (Matt 25:46). Most read this as the final judgment. Many dispensationalists place it at the start of the millennium as a judgment of the nations living through the tribulation.

Key verses: Matthew 25:31-46

### Sheol / Hades  [shared]

*Hebrew sheʾol (שְׁאוֹל); Greek hadēs (ᾅδης)* · Strong's: H7585 (Isaiah 14:9), G86 (Revelation 20:13)

The realm of the dead. In the Old Testament, Sheol is the grave and underworld for all. In Revelation, Hades is personified with Death (6:8), Christ holds their keys (1:18), they give up their dead (20:13), and both are thrown into the lake of fire (20:14).

Key verses: Isaiah 14:9-15; Revelation 1:18; Revelation 6:8; Revelation 20:13-14

### Signs of the times  [shared]

*Greek sēmeion (σημεῖον), "sign"* · Strong's: G4592 (Matthew 24:3)

Events Jesus said would mark the age before the end: deceivers, wars, famines, earthquakes, persecution, lawlessness, and the gospel reaching all nations (Matt 24:4-14). Jesus calls these "merely the beginning of birth pains" and warns, "that is not yet the end."

Key verses: Matthew 24:3-14; Luke 21:25-28

### Son of destruction (son of perdition)  [shared]

*Greek ho huios tēs apōleias (ὁ υἱὸς τῆς ἀπωλείας)* · Strong's: G684 (2 Thessalonians 2:3)

A Semitic idiom for one destined to ruin. It is used of the man of lawlessness (2 Thess 2:3) and of Judas (John 17:12).

Key verses: 2 Thessalonians 2:3; John 17:12

### Son of Man  [shared]

*Aramaic bar ʾenash (בַּר אֱנָשׁ); Greek ho huios tou anthrōpou* · Strong's: H1247 (Daniel 7:13), H606 (Daniel 7:13), G5207 (Matthew 24:30)

In Daniel 7:13, "one like a Son of Man" comes with the clouds to the Ancient of Days and receives everlasting dominion. It was Jesus' favorite self-title, and He tied His coming in glory directly to this vision (Matt 24:30; 26:64).

Key verses: Daniel 7:13-14; Matthew 24:30; Matthew 26:64; Revelation 1:13

### The apostasy (falling away)  [debated]

*Greek apostasia (ἀποστασία), "rebellion, defection"* · Strong's: G646 (2 Thessalonians 2:3)

A rebellion that must come before the Day of the Lord (2 Thess 2:3). Most see a widespread defection from the faith, and some a broader revolt against God. A minority of pretribulationists have argued that it means a "departure," the rapture, though most scholars reject this.

Key verses: 2 Thessalonians 2:3; Matthew 24:10-12; 1 Timothy 4:1

### The restrainer  [debated]

*Greek to katechon / ho katechōn (τὸ κατέχον / ὁ κατέχων)* · Strong's: G2722 (2 Thessalonians 2:6)

Something (neuter, v. 6) and someone (masculine, v. 7) that holds back the man of lawlessness until he is "taken out of the way." Proposals include the Roman Empire and the rule of law (Tertullian; many fathers), the Holy Spirit or the Spirit-indwelt church (common among dispensationalists), the preaching of the gospel, Michael the archangel, and God's sovereign decree. Augustine frankly admitted he did not know what Paul meant (City of God 20.19).

Key verses: 2 Thessalonians 2:6-7

### "This generation"  [debated]

*Greek genea (γενεά)* · Strong's: G1074 (Matthew 24:34)

Jesus said "this generation will not pass away until all these things take place" (Matt 24:34). Preterists and many others take it to mean the hearers' own generation, fulfilled in AD 70. Dispensationalists often take it to mean the generation that sees the final signs, or the Jewish people as a race.

Key verses: Matthew 24:34; Matthew 23:36

### Time, times, and half a time  [debated]

*Aramaic ʿiddan (Dan 7:25); Hebrew moʿed (Dan 12:7)*

A symbolic 3½ (one plus two plus half). It appears in Daniel 7:25 and 12:7 and Revelation 12:14, alongside 42 months (11:2; 13:5) and 1,260 days (11:3; 12:6). Futurists take it as literal 3½ years. Idealists take it as the limited period of the church's persecution, a broken seven, cut short by God.

Key verses: Daniel 7:25; Daniel 12:7; Revelation 12:14; Revelation 11:2-3

### Times of the Gentiles  [debated]

*Greek kairoi ethnōn (καιροὶ ἐθνῶν)* · Strong's: G2540 (Luke 21:24), G1484 (Luke 21:24)

"Jerusalem will be trampled under foot by the Gentiles until the times of the Gentiles are fulfilled" (Luke 21:24). This is the period of Gentile domination of Jerusalem and of Gentile ingathering, which ends with God's future dealings with Israel (compare Rom 11:25).

Key verses: Luke 21:24; Romans 11:25

### Tree of life  [shared]

*Greek xylon zōēs*

Lost in Eden (Gen 3:22-24) and restored in the New Jerusalem, where its leaves are "for the healing of the nations" (Rev 22:2). The Bible ends where it began, but now in a garden-city.

Key verses: Genesis 2:9; Genesis 3:22-24; Revelation 2:7; Revelation 22:1-2; Revelation 22:14

### Tribulation  [shared]

*Greek thlipsis (θλῖψις), "pressure, affliction"* · Strong's: G2347 (Matthew 24:9)

Affliction or distress. In the New Testament, believers face tribulation throughout this age (John 16:33; Acts 14:22; Rev 1:9). In popular usage, "the Tribulation" means a final seven-year period, Daniel's 70th week, though the New Testament never uses the word for a seven-year span.

Key verses: John 16:33; Acts 14:22; Revelation 1:9; Matthew 24:9

### Twenty-four elders  [debated]

*Greek presbyteroi (πρεσβύτεροι)* · Strong's: G4245 (Revelation 4:4)

Enthroned, crowned, white-robed elders who worship around God's throne (Rev 4:4, 10). They are seen as representatives of the whole people of God (twelve tribes plus twelve apostles, compare 21:12-14), as an angelic council, or, in many dispensational readings, as the raptured church.

Key verses: Revelation 4:4; Revelation 4:10-11; Revelation 5:8-10; 1 Chronicles 24:1-19

### Two witnesses  [debated]

*Greek dyo martyres (δύο μάρτυρες)* · Strong's: G3144 (Revelation 11:3)

Two prophets who prophesy 1,260 days in sackcloth, are called "the two olive trees and the two lampstands" (Zech 4), work Moses- and Elijah-like signs, are killed by the beast, lie unburied in "the great city," and rise after 3½ days (Rev 11:3-13). Futurists expect two individuals, perhaps Moses and Elijah or Enoch and Elijah (an early tradition found in Hippolytus and Tertullian). Idealists see the witnessing church, since lampstands stand for churches in 1:20.

Key verses: Revelation 11:3-13; Zechariah 4:1-14; Malachi 4:5; Revelation 1:20

### Wrath of God / wrath of the Lamb  [shared]

*Greek orgē (ὀργή)* · Strong's: G3709 (Revelation 6:17), G3709 (1 Thessalonians 5:9)

God's righteous judgment on sin. "God has not appointed us for wrath" (1 Thess 5:9). Views differ on whether this promise requires removal from the earth (pretribulation) or protection through judgments on earth, as Israel was protected in Goshen (Exod 8:22).

Key verses: 1 Thessalonians 1:10; 1 Thessalonians 5:9; Revelation 6:16-17; Romans 5:9

## The main views (7)

### Dispensational premillennialism (pretribulation)

Christ comes for His church before a seven-year tribulation (the rapture), then returns with His church after it to judge the beast and set up a literal thousand-year kingdom on earth, centered in Jerusalem, where God's unconditional promises to national Israel are fulfilled. After a final revolt, the great white throne judgment and the eternal state follow. The key commitments are a consistently literal reading of prophecy and a lasting distinction between Israel and the church.

**Order of events**

1. Church age (the "mystery" parenthesis between Daniel's 69th and 70th weeks)
2. Pretribulation rapture: the dead in Christ rise and living believers are caught up (1 Thess 4:16-17)
3. Judgment seat of Christ and marriage of the Lamb in heaven
4. The Antichrist confirms a seven-year covenant with Israel (Dan 9:27)
5. First 3½ years: seals, the 144,000 sealed, the two witnesses
6. Midpoint: abomination of desolation in a rebuilt temple; the Antichrist claims deity
7. Great tribulation (3½ years): trumpets, bowls, the mark of the beast
8. Armageddon; Christ returns with His saints (Rev 19)
9. Israel's national repentance (Zech 12:10); judgment of the nations (Matt 25)
10. Satan bound; tribulation saints raised; the 1,000-year reign from Jerusalem
11. Satan released; Gog and Magog revolt crushed (Rev 20:7-10)
12. Great white throne judgment of the unbelieving dead
13. New heavens and new earth; the New Jerusalem

**Key texts:** 1 Thessalonians 4:13-18; Daniel 9:24-27; Revelation 3:10; Revelation 19:11-20:6; Romans 11:25-29; Zechariah 14:1-11; Ezekiel 40-48

**Who holds it**

- **John Nelson Darby (1800-1882)**: Plymouth Brethren founder and systematizer of dispensationalism and the pretribulation rapture
- **C. I. Scofield**: The Scofield Reference Bible (1909; rev. 1917)
- **Lewis Sperry Chafer**: Systematic Theology (8 vols., 1947-48); founder of Dallas Theological Seminary
- **John F. Walvoord**: The Rapture Question (1957); The Millennial Kingdom (1959); The Revelation of Jesus Christ (1966); Daniel: The Key to Prophetic Revelation (1971)
- **J. Dwight Pentecost**: Things to Come (1958)
- **Charles C. Ryrie**: Dispensationalism Today (1965), revised as Dispensationalism (1995; 2007): the three "sine qua non"
- **Paul D. Feinberg**: the pretribulation case in Reiter, ed., Three Views on the Rapture (1984)
- **Arnold Fruchtenbaum**: The Footsteps of the Messiah (1982; rev. 2003)
- **John MacArthur**: The Second Coming (1999); MacArthur New Testament Commentary: Revelation 1-11 (1999) and 12-22 (2000); the 2007 Shepherds' Conference message "Why Every Self-Respecting Calvinist Is a Premillennialist"
- **Craig Blaising and Darrell Bock**: Progressive Dispensationalism (1993), a revised form
- **Popular works**: Hal Lindsey, The Late Great Planet Earth (1970); Tim LaHaye and Jerry B. Jenkins, the Left Behind novels (1995-2007)

**Strengths**

- Takes Old Testament land and kingdom promises to Israel at face value (Rom 11:29, "the gifts and the calling of God are irrevocable").
- Gives Daniel 9:27 a coherent future setting.
- Reads Revelation 19-20 in sequence: return, then binding, then a thousand years.
- Strong emphasis on readiness and imminence.

**Hard questions**

- No text clearly separates a rapture from the visible coming by seven years. Matt 24:29-31 and 2 Thess 2:1-3 place the gathering "after the tribulation."
- The 2,000-year gap between Daniel's 69th and 70th weeks is not stated in the text.
- The system is historically recent (1830s onward), though its defenders note earlier hints.
- Would Gentile believers be second-class or separate from Israel's promises (Eph 2:14-16; Gal 3:29)? Progressive dispensationalism tries to answer this.

**Solomon's assessment:** Dispensationalism has given the church zeal for prophecy, love for Israel, and serious attention to the text. I am not persuaded by the pretribulation rapture. The New Testament presents one visible coming that gathers the saints after the tribulation (Matt 24:29-31; 2 Thess 2:1-8). But this is a family disagreement among Bible-believing Christians, not a test of orthodoxy.

### Historic premillennialism

Christ returns once, after a final period of tribulation that the church passes through, to raise the righteous dead, catch up living believers to meet Him, destroy the Antichrist, and reign on earth for a thousand years (whether literal or not). Then come the final rebellion, the resurrection of the rest of the dead, the last judgment, and the new creation. There is no Israel/church split: the church is the one people of God, though many expect a future large-scale conversion of ethnic Israel.

**Order of events**

1. Kingdom inaugurated in Christ's first coming (already/not yet)
2. Gospel to all nations; growing apostasy
3. Antichrist revealed; great tribulation; the church persecuted and preserved
4. Christ's one visible return; the righteous dead raised; living saints caught up to meet Him in the air and return with Him (1 Thess 4:17)
5. Antichrist destroyed (2 Thess 2:8); Satan bound
6. Christ reigns with His saints on earth (Rev 20:4-6)
7. Satan released; final rebellion crushed
8. Resurrection of the rest of the dead; great white throne judgment
9. New heavens and new earth

**Key texts:** Revelation 19:11-20:6; 1 Corinthians 15:22-26; Matthew 24:29-31; 2 Thessalonians 2:1-8; Isaiah 65:17-25

**Who holds it**

- **Papias (c. 60-130)**: Fragments preserved in Irenaeus, Against Heresies 5.33.3-4, and Eusebius, Church History 3.39
- **Justin Martyr**: Dialogue with Trypho 80-81 (c. 155-160): a thousand years in a rebuilt Jerusalem, while admitting many true Christians thought otherwise
- **Irenaeus of Lyons**: Against Heresies 5.25-36 (c. 180): Antichrist, 666, and the kingdom of the righteous after the resurrection
- **Tertullian**: Against Marcion 3.24
- **Hippolytus of Rome**: On Christ and Antichrist (c. 200); Commentary on Daniel
- **George Eldon Ladd**: The Blessed Hope (1956); A Commentary on the Revelation of John (1972); The Presence of the Future (1974); "Historic Premillennialism" in Robert Clouse, ed., The Meaning of the Millennium: Four Views (1977)
- **Robert H. Gundry**: The Church and the Tribulation (1973), posttribulational
- **Douglas Moo**: the posttribulation case in Three Views on the Rapture (1984; 2010)
- **Craig Blomberg and Sung Wook Chung, eds.**: A Case for Historic Premillennialism (2009)
- **William Lane Craig**: Defenders class "Doctrine of Last Things" (Reasonable Faith): argues against a secret pretribulation rapture from Mark 13 and 1 Thess 4. On the millennium, he surveys the views and says it is not an area he has studied in depth

**Strengths**

- The plainest sequential reading of Revelation 19-20 (return, then a thousand-year reign).
- The earliest recorded millennial view in the church (Papias, Justin, Irenaeus).
- Avoids a two-stage return. Fits Matthew 24:29-31 and 2 Thessalonians 2.
- Explains 1 Corinthians 15:23-24 ("each in his own order... then comes the end") as stages with an interval.

**Hard questions**

- Why would glorified saints live alongside mortal nations who later rebel?
- The rest of the New Testament seems to place one resurrection and judgment together at the return (John 5:28-29; Matt 25:31-46; 2 Thess 1:7-10).
- Revelation is highly symbolic. Is 19-20 chronological or recapitulating?

**Solomon's assessment:** Historic premillennialism has the oldest witness in the post-apostolic church and reads Revelation 20:4-6 straightforwardly ("they came to life"). It also keeps the one, posttribulational return that the rest of the New Testament teaches. I find it a strong, honest option.

### Amillennialism

The thousand years of Revelation 20 is a symbol for the present age, from Christ's first coming to His second. Satan is bound now, so that he cannot stop the gospel going to the nations (Matt 12:29). Believers who die reign with Christ in heaven (the "first resurrection"). Good and evil grow together until the end, with a final intensification of tribulation and the Antichrist. Christ then returns once, the dead are raised all at once, and the last judgment and new creation follow immediately. Revelation is read as a series of parallel visions of the whole age.

**Order of events**

1. Christ's victory at the cross binds Satan (Matt 12:29; John 12:31; Rev 20:1-3)
2. The thousand years: the church age; departed saints reign with Christ (Rev 20:4-6)
3. Gospel to the nations; tribulation and apostasy throughout the age
4. A brief final crisis: Satan "released for a short time"; the man of lawlessness; Gog and Magog (Rev 20:7-9 = 16:14 = 19:19)
5. Christ's one visible return
6. General resurrection of righteous and wicked (John 5:28-29)
7. Final judgment (Matt 25:31-46; Rev 20:11-15)
8. New heavens and new earth

**Key texts:** Revelation 20:1-6; Matthew 12:28-29; John 5:24-29; Matthew 13:24-43; 2 Thessalonians 1:6-10; 2 Peter 3:10-13

**Who holds it**

- **Tyconius (late 4th century)**: Book of Rules, which influenced Augustine's reading of Revelation
- **Augustine of Hippo**: City of God 20.6-9 (c. 426): the first resurrection is spiritual, the thousand years is the church age, the saints reign now. In 20.7 he admits he once held a millennial view
- **John Calvin**: Institutes 3.25.5 rejects the chiliasts' thousand years as too short a reign for Christ
- **William Hendriksen**: More Than Conquerors (1940): progressive parallelism in seven sections
- **Anthony Hoekema**: The Bible and the Future (1979); "Amillennialism" in Clouse, ed. (1977)
- **Robert Strimple**: the amillennial case in Bock, ed., Three Views on the Millennium and Beyond (1999)
- **Kim Riddlebarger**: A Case for Amillennialism (2003; exp. 2013); The Man of Sin (2006)
- **G. K. Beale**: The Book of Revelation, NIGTC (1999); with David Campbell, Revelation: A Shorter Commentary (2015)
- **Sam Storms**: Kingdom Come: The Amillennial Alternative (2013). He moved to amillennialism from premillennialism
- **Confessional tradition**: Most Lutheran, Reformed, Anglican, and Orthodox traditions; the Catholic Catechism (§676) rejects millenarianism

**Strengths**

- Matches the New Testament's general pattern: one return, one resurrection, one judgment (John 5:28-29; Acts 24:15; 2 Thess 1:7-10).
- Explains the binding of Satan by Jesus' own words (Matt 12:29) and the gospel's spread.
- Recognizes Revelation's recapitulating structure (three "final" battles that use the same language: 16:14, 19:19, 20:8).
- Has been the majority view of the church since Augustine.

**Hard questions**

- Does "they came to life" (ezēsan) in Rev 20:4 really shift meaning from 20:5 ("the rest of the dead did not come to life")? (Ladd's main objection.)
- Is Satan really "bound" when 1 Peter 5:8 describes him prowling like a lion?
- How are the Old Testament promises of earthly peace (Isa 65:20, with its death at 100 years) fulfilled?

**Solomon's assessment:** Amillennialism has the best explanation of the New Testament's one-return, one-resurrection pattern, and it reads Revelation as John's first readers likely heard it: as a book of repeated, deepening visions. Augustine, much of the Reformation, and many careful exegetes (Hoekema, Beale) stand here. On balance, this is the reading I find best supported, but I hold it humbly. Ladd's objection about "came to life" is serious.

### Postmillennialism

Christ returns after (post) the millennium, a long era in which the gospel, by the Spirit's power, converts the great majority of the world and transforms cultures, while Christ reigns from heaven. The "thousand years" is a long golden age (for some, the whole church age gradually rising to it). Near the end, Satan's brief revolt fails, and Christ returns once for the general resurrection and judgment. Many postmillennialists are also partial preterists: they see the great tribulation and much of Revelation fulfilled in AD 70.

**Order of events**

1. Christ enthroned at the Father's right hand "Until I put Your enemies as a footstool for Your feet" (Ps 110:1)
2. (Partial preterists: the great tribulation and the "coming" in judgment on Jerusalem, AD 70)
3. The Great Commission prospers; the mustard seed and leaven grow (Matt 13:31-33)
4. The millennial golden age: most nations converted; justice and peace spread (Isa 2:2-4)
5. Satan's brief release; final rebellion
6. Christ's one visible return; general resurrection; final judgment
7. New heavens and new earth

**Key texts:** Psalm 110:1-2; Psalm 2:8; Matthew 13:31-33; Matthew 28:18-20; 1 Corinthians 15:24-26; Isaiah 2:2-4; Daniel 2:35; Habakkuk 2:14

**Who holds it**

- **Jonathan Edwards**: A History of the Work of Redemption (preached 1739, published 1774)
- **The Puritan hope**: Iain H. Murray, The Puritan Hope (1971), a history of Puritan postmillennial expectation
- **Charles Hodge and B. B. Warfield**: Hodge, Systematic Theology vol. 3 (1872-73); Warfield, "The Millennium and the Apocalypse" (1904)
- **Loraine Boettner**: The Millennium (1957); "Postmillennialism" in Clouse, ed. (1977)
- **Kenneth L. Gentry Jr.**: He Shall Have Dominion (1992; 3rd ed. 2009); Before Jerusalem Fell (1989); The Beast of Revelation (1989); the postmillennial case in Bock, ed., Three Views (1999); The Divorce of Israel (2016)
- **Keith A. Mathison**: Postmillennialism: An Eschatology of Hope (1999)
- **Douglas Wilson**: Heaven Misplaced: Christ's Kingdom on Earth (2008); When the Man Comes Around (2019), a Revelation commentary
- **Panel**: Wilson (postmillennial), Sam Storms (amillennial), and Jim Hamilton (historic premillennial) debated in a Desiring God panel moderated by John Piper

**Strengths**

- Takes Christ's present authority (Matt 28:18) and the growth parables seriously.
- Joyful confidence in the gospel's success (Hab 2:14; Isa 11:9).
- Historically fueled the modern missionary movement (William Carey was postmillennial).

**Hard questions**

- Jesus warned of persecution and apostasy to the end (Matt 24:9-12; Luke 18:8; 2 Tim 3:1-5).
- If the tribulation was AD 70, what is the final apostasy of 2 Thessalonians 2 and Revelation 20:7-9?
- Was it discredited by the 20th century's wars? (Postmillennialists answer that history is long and the kingdom grows slowly.)

**Solomon's assessment:** Postmillennialism rightly refuses defeatism: Christ is reigning now and His gospel will fill the earth with the knowledge of the Lord. My hesitation is that the New Testament expects suffering and deception to run alongside gospel growth until the end (Matt 13:30, "allow both to grow together until the harvest"). Still, its hope is a needed corrective to fearful prophecy-watching.

### Preterism (partial and full)

Preterism is a way of reading prophecy rather than a millennial position. Partial preterists hold that the Olivet Discourse's tribulation and much of Revelation (often 4-19) were fulfilled in the Jewish war and the destruction of Jerusalem (AD 66-70), with Nero as the beast. They base this on the time texts ("this generation," "the time is near," Rev 1:3). They still expect a future bodily return, resurrection, and judgment, and are usually postmillennial or amillennial. Full (hyper-) preterists say everything, including the Second Coming and resurrection, happened by AD 70. That is outside creedal orthodoxy.

**Order of events**

1. Jesus predicts the temple's fall within "this generation" (Matt 24:2, 34)
2. False christs, wars, famines, persecution (AD 30-66)
3. Abomination of desolation: Jerusalem surrounded by armies (Luke 21:20)
4. Great tribulation: the siege of Jerusalem, AD 66-70
5. The "coming of the Son of Man" as a judgment-coming on Jerusalem (Dan 7:13 imagery)
6. The beast = Nero and Rome (666 = Nero Caesar)
7. Church age: Christ reigns and the gospel advances
8. Partial preterists: future bodily return, resurrection, and last judgment (Acts 1:11; 1 Cor 15)
9. New heavens and new earth

**Key texts:** Matthew 24:34; Matthew 16:28; Revelation 1:1-3; Revelation 22:6-10; Luke 21:20-24; Matthew 23:36-38

**Who holds it**

- **Luis de Alcázar (Jesuit)**: Vestigatio arcani sensus in Apocalypsi (1614), an early systematic preterist commentary
- **Eusebius**: Church History 3.5-8: treats the fall of Jerusalem as fulfilling Christ's predictions (quoting Josephus)
- **R. C. Sproul**: The Last Days According to Jesus (1998): engages J. Stuart Russell's full preterism and argues for a partial-preterist reading of the Olivet Discourse and the time texts, while affirming a future return and resurrection
- **Kenneth L. Gentry Jr.**: Before Jerusalem Fell (1989), which argues Revelation was written before AD 70; "A Preterist View of Revelation" in C. Marvin Pate, ed., Four Views on the Book of Revelation (1998)
- **Gary DeMar**: Last Days Madness (1991)
- **Douglas Wilson**: When the Man Comes Around (2019)
- **Wise Disciple (Nate)**: YouTube video "Is This the End? Israel-Iran War & What Revelation Really Says": warns against assigning modern nations to Gog and Magog, and reads much of Matthew 24 as fulfilled in the first century (he cites D. A. Carson on Jewish views of "this age")
- **D. A. Carson**: "Matthew," Expositor's Bible Commentary (1984; rev. 2010). Not a preterist, but takes the AD 70 dimension of Matthew 24 seriously. He reads the discourse as answering both the temple question and the end question
- **Full preterism (not orthodox)**: J. Stuart Russell, The Parousia (1878); Max R. King, The Spirit of Prophecy (1971). Critiqued in Keith Mathison, ed., When Shall These Things Be? A Reformed Response to Hyper-Preterism (2004)

**Strengths**

- Takes the time texts seriously ("this generation"; "the time is near"; "the things which must soon happen," Rev 1:1).
- Revelation was addressed to seven real churches facing real persecution.
- AD 70 was a world-shaking fulfillment that Jesus clearly foretold (Luke 19:41-44; 21:20-24).

**Hard questions**

- Requires an early date for Revelation (before AD 70). Most scholars, following Irenaeus (Against Heresies 5.30.3), date it to about AD 95 under Domitian.
- Some "cosmic" language (resurrection, the new creation, every eye seeing Him) does not fit AD 70.
- Full preterism denies the future bodily resurrection, which the creeds confess and Paul treats as essential (1 Cor 15:12-19; 2 Tim 2:18).

**Solomon's assessment:** Partial preterism has helped me see how much of Matthew 24 was a real warning to the apostles' generation, and how Revelation spoke to the seven churches. I think AD 70 is a true but partial fulfillment that previews the end, and that Revelation's horizon reaches beyond it. Full preterism must be rejected: the resurrection of the body is not behind us.

### Four ways to read Revelation

Separate from the millennial question is how Revelation's visions relate to time. Preterist: fulfilled mostly in the first century. Historicist: a map of church history. Futurist: mostly still future. Idealist: timeless truths about the spiritual war. Most modern commentators blend these ("eclectic").

**Order of events**

1. Preterist: Revelation 4-19 fulfilled by AD 70 or the fall of Rome
2. Historicist: seals, trumpets, and bowls trace the church from Rome to the Reformation and on
3. Futurist: chapters 4-22 describe the final years before and after the return
4. Idealist: the visions picture recurring realities in every age
5. Eclectic: first-century address + every-age pattern + future climax

**Key texts:** Revelation 1:1-3; Revelation 1:19; Revelation 4:1; Revelation 22:10

**Who holds it**

- **Steve Gregg**: Revelation: Four Views, A Parallel Commentary (1997; rev. 2013), which sets the four approaches side by side
- **C. Marvin Pate, ed.**: Four Views on the Book of Revelation (1998): Gentry (preterist), Sam Hamstra Jr. (idealist), Pate (progressive dispensational), Robert Thomas (classical dispensational)
- **Richard Bauckham**: The Theology of the Book of Revelation (1993); The Climax of Prophecy (1993)
- **G. K. Beale**: NIGTC Revelation (1999), eclectic modified idealism with a future consummation
- **Grant Osborne**: Revelation, BECNT (2002), eclectic with a futurist leaning
- **Robert Mounce**: The Book of Revelation, NICNT (1977; rev. 1998)
- **Isaac Newton; E. B. Elliott**: historicist classics (1733; 1844)
- **Francisco Ribera; Luis de Alcázar**: Jesuit futurist (c. 1590) and preterist (1614) commentaries

**Strengths**

- Preterist: honors the original audience. Historicist: sees God at work in history. Futurist: takes the climax seriously. Idealist: makes the book usable in every age.

**Hard questions**

- Preterist: can shrink the final consummation. Historicist: its timelines keep needing revision and are Western-centered. Futurist: can make the book irrelevant to its first readers. Idealist: can lose the real future.

**Solomon's assessment:** I read Revelation eclectically, as Beale and Bauckham do. It was a letter to real first-century churches about Rome's beastly power. It shows patterns that recur in every age. And it ends in a still-future return, resurrection, judgment, and new creation. For a novelist, that is freeing: the beast's pattern can recur in a future AI-era empire without claiming you've decoded the prophecy.

### When is the rapture?

All Christians affirm that believers alive at Christ's coming will be "caught up... to meet the Lord in the air" (1 Thess 4:17). The question is when, relative to the tribulation, and whether this is a separate event from His visible coming.

**Order of events**

1. Pretribulation: Christ comes for the church before the 70th week; returns with it seven years later
2. Midtribulation: at the midpoint, at the "last trumpet"
3. Pre-wrath: after the abomination and great tribulation begin, at the sixth seal's cosmic signs, before the trumpets and bowls (God's wrath)
4. Posttribulation: one coming after the tribulation; the church meets Him in the air and escorts Him to earth

**Key texts:** 1 Thessalonians 4:13-18; 1 Thessalonians 5:1-9; 2 Thessalonians 2:1-8; Matthew 24:29-31; 1 Corinthians 15:51-52; Revelation 3:10; John 14:1-3

**Who holds it**

- **Pre**: Walvoord, The Rapture Question (1957); Pentecost; Ryrie; MacArthur; Feinberg (1984); Blaising (2010)
- **Mid**: Gleason Archer, in Three Views on the Rapture (1984)
- **Pre-wrath**: Marvin Rosenthal (1990); Robert Van Kampen (1992); Alan Hultberg (2010)
- **Post**: Ladd, The Blessed Hope (1956); Gundry (1973); Moo (1984; 2010); William Lane Craig (Defenders, "Doctrine of Last Things"); and functionally all amillennialists and postmillennialists

**Strengths**

- Pre: comfort, imminence, and the distinction between wrath and tribulation. Post: the simplest reading of Matt 24:29-31 and 2 Thess 2. Pre-wrath: takes the 'wrath' language of Rev 6:16-17 seriously.

**Hard questions**

- Pre: no text clearly says two comings seven years apart. Post: how is the church spared wrath? (Answer: protection, as Israel was spared in Goshen.) Mid and pre-wrath: they depend on precise placement of trumpet and wrath language.

**Solomon's assessment:** I believe the rapture is the church's joyful meeting with the Lord at His one visible coming after the tribulation. The word for "meet" (apantēsis) described citizens going out to welcome an arriving king and escort him into the city (compare Acts 28:15). But whatever our timing view, the apostle's application is the same for all: "comfort one another with these words" (1 Thess 4:18).

## Events (40)

### 1. Christ's first coming inaugurates the last days  [Already; shared]

The New Testament says the "last days" began with Jesus' life, death, resurrection, ascension, and the outpouring of the Spirit. The future is anchored in what God has already done.

References: Hebrews 1:1-2; Acts 2:16-17; 1 Corinthians 10:11; Matthew 12:28

- **Dispensational:** Agrees that the church age is the "last days," but sees prophecies about Israel's kingdom as postponed.
- **Historic premil:** The kingdom has been inaugurated and will be consummated in an earthly millennium.
- **Amillennial:** The kingdom has been inaugurated, and the millennium is this present age.
- **Postmillennial:** The kingdom has been inaugurated and will grow until it fills the earth (Dan 2:35).
- **Preterist reading:** The "last days" were mainly the last days of the old covenant era, ending in AD 70.

### 2. Beginning of birth pains  [Signs; debated]

False christs, wars, famines, and earthquakes. Jesus says, "See that you are not alarmed" and calls them "merely the beginning of birth pains."

References: Matthew 24:4-8; Mark 13:5-8; Luke 21:8-11

- **Dispensational:** General signs that intensify in the future tribulation; some place them in the first half of the 70th week.
- **Historic premil:** Marks of the whole age that grow more intense before the end.
- **Amillennial:** Marks of the whole age between Christ's two comings.
- **Postmillennial:** Fulfilled in large part before AD 70; such troubles do not stop the gospel's advance.
- **Preterist reading:** Fulfilled before AD 70: false messiahs, the famine under Claudius (Acts 11:28), wars, and earthquakes.

### 3. Persecution and the gospel to all nations  [Signs; shared]

"This gospel of the kingdom shall be proclaimed in the whole world as a witness to all the nations, and then the end will come" (Matt 24:14).

References: Matthew 24:9-14; Mark 13:9-13; Revelation 6:9-11

- **Dispensational:** Completed in part by the 144,000 and angelic preaching during the tribulation (Rev 14:6).
- **Historic premil:** Mission to every people must be completed before the end.
- **Amillennial:** The church's present mission; the end waits for it.
- **Postmillennial:** Ensures global conversion of the nations before Christ returns.
- **Preterist reading:** Fulfilled in the apostolic mission to the known world (Col 1:6, 23), then continues.

### 4. Fall of Jerusalem and the temple (AD 70)  [History; shared]

Historical fact: Roman armies under Titus destroyed Jerusalem and the temple in AD 70, as Jesus foretold. Josephus (Jewish War, books 5-6) is the main ancient account. The debate is how much of the Olivet Discourse refers to it.

References: Matthew 24:1-2; Luke 19:41-44; Luke 21:20-24

- **Dispensational:** Luke 21:20-24 fulfilled in AD 70; most of Matthew 24 still future.
- **Historic premil:** A partial fulfillment and foreshadowing of the end.
- **Amillennial:** A real fulfillment and a type of the final judgment.
- **Postmillennial:** The great tribulation of Matthew 24:15-28; a central fulfillment.
- **Preterist reading:** The "coming" of the Son of Man in judgment on Jerusalem (Matt 24:30, read with Dan 7:13 imagery).

### 5. The great apostasy  [Signs; debated]

A falling away or rebellion must come before the Day of the Lord.

References: 2 Thessalonians 2:3; Matthew 24:10-12; 1 Timothy 4:1-3; 2 Timothy 3:1-5

- **Dispensational:** A future, global departure from the faith; a minority reads apostasia as the rapture ("departure").
- **Historic premil:** A future defection just before the return.
- **Amillennial:** Recurring across the age; peaks just before the end.
- **Postmillennial:** Some place it in the first century (the Jewish revolt or apostasy), others before the end.
- **Preterist reading:** The Jewish revolt against Rome or the first-century apostasy (Gentry).

### 6. The restrainer removed  [Signs; debated]

What restrains the lawless one is "taken out of the way," and then he is revealed.

References: 2 Thessalonians 2:6-7

- **Dispensational:** The Spirit's restraining work through the church is removed at the rapture.
- **Historic premil:** Proposals vary: government, gospel preaching, Michael.
- **Amillennial:** God's providential restraint (often government and law) is lifted near the end.
- **Postmillennial:** Often identified with first-century powers, such as the Roman order or Claudius.
- **Preterist reading:** A first-century restraint, such as Rome or Claudius (Gentry), lifted before Nero's persecution.

### 7. Rapture (gathering of the elect)  [Return; shared]

Living believers are "caught up together with them in the clouds to meet the Lord in the air" (1 Thess 4:17). Every view affirms the event; its timing is the dispute.

References: 1 Thessalonians 4:13-18; 1 Corinthians 15:51-52; Matthew 24:31; John 14:1-3

- **Dispensational:** Pretribulational: a separate coming for the church before the 70th week.
- **Historic premil:** Posttribulational: at the one Second Coming, the church meets the Lord and returns with Him.
- **Amillennial:** Part of the one Second Coming, together with the general resurrection.
- **Postmillennial:** Part of the one Second Coming at the end of history.
- **Preterist reading:** Future, at the one Second Coming (partial preterists); full preterists spiritualize it, which is not orthodox.

### 8. The covenant and the 70th week begin  [Tribulation; debated]

"He will make a firm covenant with the many for one week" (Dan 9:27).

References: Daniel 9:27

- **Dispensational:** The Antichrist signs a seven-year treaty with Israel; the tribulation begins.
- **Historic premil:** Many hold to a final seven years; some are less precise.
- **Amillennial:** Usually Christ confirms the covenant; the 70th week is fulfilled or symbolic.
- **Postmillennial:** Christ confirms the new covenant; His death ends sacrifice in the middle of the week.
- **Preterist reading:** Fulfilled in Christ's ministry and the events leading to AD 70.

### 9. A rebuilt temple  [Tribulation; debated]

Many futurists expect a rebuilt Jerusalem temple where sacrifices resume and the lawless one sits. No text explicitly predicts a third temple. The expectation is inferred from 2 Thess 2:4, Rev 11:1-2, and Dan 9:27.

References: 2 Thessalonians 2:4; Revelation 11:1-2; Ezekiel 40-48; Daniel 9:27

- **Dispensational:** A literal tribulation temple and a millennial temple (Ezek 40-48).
- **Historic premil:** Some expect one; others read the "temple" in 2 Thess 2:4 as the church or a figure of speech.
- **Amillennial:** "Temple of God" means the church (1 Cor 3:16) or is figurative, and the lawless one usurps God's place among His people.
- **Postmillennial:** Refers to the Herodian temple before AD 70.
- **Preterist reading:** Fulfilled in the Herodian temple before AD 70.

### 10. Abomination of desolation  [Tribulation; debated]

Jesus said: "when you see the abomination of desolation... standing in the holy place... flee to the mountains" (Matt 24:15-16). The first fulfillment was Antiochus IV in 167 BC.

References: Daniel 9:27; Daniel 11:31; Daniel 12:11; Matthew 24:15; Luke 21:20

- **Dispensational:** Antichrist's desecration of a rebuilt temple at the midpoint of the seven years.

### 1. Surveillance: two kinds of seeing

Scripture's all-seeing beings are God and His throne-servants. The living creatures are "full of eyes" (Rev 4:6-8), and the Lamb's seven eyes are the Spirit "sent out into all the earth" (5:6). "There is no creature hidden from His sight" (Heb 4:13). God's seeing is holy and personal, and it ends in either judgment or rescue.

Revelation 13 never says the beast watches. It does say the beast's regime can tell who has the mark and who does not, and can exclude and kill. That requires knowing. A surveillance AI is a reasonable fictional picture of a counterfeit omniscience, eyes that watch to control, set against the eyes that see to save.

### 2. Economic control

"No one will be able to buy or to sell, except the one who has the mark" (13:17). In first-century Asia Minor, trade guilds held feasts for patron gods and the emperor, and refusing them could cost a Christian their livelihood (compare 2:14, 20 on "things sacrificed to idols"). Revelation 18 then exposes the luxury economy of Babylon, which ends with "human beings and human lives" as cargo (18:13). Richard Bauckham's chapter "The Economic Critique of Rome in Revelation 18" (The Climax of Prophecy, 1993) is the standard study.

For the novel, the text's pattern is clear: loyalty to the beast is enforced economically, and faithful people pay a real price. The pattern matters more than any one technology. Do not identify a real currency, company, or government as the fulfillment.

### 3. The mark

The mark (charagma, G5480) is on the right hand or forehead and is "either the name of the beast or the number of his name" (13:17). It is a counterfeit of God's seal on His servants' foreheads (7:3; 9:4; 14:1; 22:4), which itself echoes Deuteronomy 6:8 (God's words bound on hand and forehead) and Ezekiel 9:4 (a mark on those who grieve over sin). So the two marks show two allegiances: the hand stands for what you do, the forehead for what you think and who you belong to.

Futurists often expect a literal, visible mark tied to commerce. Many others (Beale, Bauckham) see a symbol of allegiance shown in conduct. Both readings agree on the main point: the mark is bound up with worship, and Revelation warns that receiving it is a final choice (14:9-11). Every technology that has been declared to be the mark (credit cards, barcodes, Social Security numbers, microchips, vaccines) is speculation, and good Christians have been needlessly frightened by such claims.

Fiction idea (speculation): Your story can make the mark something people choose with open eyes, for example an interface you accept by pledging loyalty to the system or its image. That keeps it true to the text: it is never taken by accident.

### 4. Deception, signs, and the return that cannot be faked

Jesus' first word in the Olivet Discourse is "See to it that no one deceives you" (Matt 24:4). The false prophet's power is persuasion backed by signs (Rev 13:13-14), and Paul speaks of "signs and false wonders" (2 Thess 2:9). Deuteronomy 13:1-3 already taught that a sign that "comes true" does not prove a prophet who leads you after other gods. The Didache (16.4, early 2nd century or earlier) warns that "the world-deceiver" will appear "as the Son of God" and do "signs and wonders" (Roberts-Donaldson translation).

Here AI is a natural fit for fiction: synthetic voices, generated miracles, endless plausible answers. Jesus gives His people a simple, technology-proof rule: "if they say to you, 'Behold, He is in the wilderness,' do not go out, or, 'Behold, He is in the inner rooms,' do not believe them" (Matt 24:26). His real coming is like lightning from east to west (24:27) and "every eye will see Him" (Rev 1:7).

Wise Disciple (Nate) answers Joe Rogan's suggestion that AI could make people doubt a filmed return of Jesus. In "Joe Rogan Thinks AI Could Hide the Return of Jesus," he argues from Acts 1:11 and 1 Corinthians 15:51-52 that the return will be visible, universal, and decisive, not a clip debated online. That is a good, text-based answer, and a novelist can build on it: deepfakes can imitate false christs, but not the true Christ.

### 5. The image that speaks

Revelation 13:14-15 is the closest Scripture comes to describing an artificial "living" thing. The beast from the earth tells people "to make an image to the beast," and "it was given to him to give breath to the image." The image speaks and has those who refuse to worship it killed. The word for image is eikōn (G1504). In the Greek Old Testament, humanity is made "in the image" (kat' eikona) of God (Gen 1:26-27), and Christ is "the image of the invisible God" (Col 1:15). The beast's image is a parody: a made thing, given breath by a false prophet, demanding worship.

First-century background: the imperial cult set up statues of the emperor in Asia Minor (Pergamum, Smyrna, Ephesus). Ancient writers describe staged "wonders" and talking oracles. Lucian's Alexander the False Prophet (2nd century) describes a fake serpent oracle that seemed to speak. Daniel 3 offers the pattern: an image, music, everyone bows, or the furnace.

The prophets mocked idols as dead: "They have mouths, but they do not speak" (Ps 115:5), and Habakkuk adds, "there is no breath at all inside it" (Hab 2:19). Revelation's final idol seems to break that rule, because it speaks. But the text stresses that the breath was given (edothē). It is permitted, not self-generated, and it ends in the lake of fire with its makers.

Fiction idea (speculation): An AI image that speaks, built by human hands, honored on pain of exclusion or death, is a faithful fictional use of this text if (1) it remains an image, a counterfeit and not a true person, (2) its power is shown as permitted and limited, and (3) the story's moral center is the refusal to worship, as in Daniel 3 and Revelation 20:4.

### 6. What makes us human (and what AI is not)

"God created man in His own image" (Gen 1:27). Human dignity comes from God's image, not from intelligence. A machine may imitate human speech, but in a biblical story it cannot bear God's image or receive the Spirit's new birth. The serpent's oldest promise, "you will be like God" (Gen 3:5), is the root of transhumanism, the hope of making ourselves gods. The biblical hope is resurrection, not uploading: "there will no longer be any death" (Rev 21:4) because Christ holds the keys (1:18).

"Those who make them will become like them" (Ps 115:8). The deeper danger is not that machines become persons, but that people who worship their creations become less human. That is a rich moral arc for a novel.

### 7. John Lennox, 2084

John C. Lennox (Oxford mathematician) wrote 2084: Artificial Intelligence and the Future of Humanity (Zondervan, 2020), updated and expanded as 2084 and the AI Revolution (Zondervan, 2024). The updated edition has chapters titled "Future Shock: The Return of the Man Who Is God," "Homo Deus in the Book of Revelation," and "The Time of the End" (chs. 15-17).

In the Revelation chapter, he reads Revelation 13 alongside Daniel 7 and 2 Thessalonians 2. He treats the two beasts as oppressive political authority and its propaganda, and he gives special attention to the speaking image, the punishment of those who refuse, and the restriction on buying and selling. He compares these with AI surveillance, social credit, biometric control, and AI scenarios from Max Tegmark and Yuval Noah Harari. He suggests that the image could be something like an AI-powered artificial god. He does not claim any present system fulfills the text. He presents Revelation as a warning about technology serving concentrated power, and he ends with Christ's return and resurrection hope rather than timetables. (This summary is paraphrased from reviews and the book's table of contents. I could not check the exact page wording, so I am not quoting him directly.)

Lennox's earlier book Against the Flow: The Inspiration of Daniel in an Age of Relativism (2015) is also useful for a novel that leans on Daniel.

### 9. Earlier Christian fiction about the end

Robert Hugh Benson, Lord of the World (1907): a charismatic humanitarian statesman becomes the Antichrist figure in a secular, humanist future world.

Vladimir Solovyov, "A Short Tale of the Antichrist," in Three Conversations (1900): the Antichrist is a brilliant benefactor and author of a book that wins over the world.

C. S. Lewis, That Hideous Strength (1945): a scientific institute (N.I.C.E.) serves dark powers through a preserved, speaking "Head." It is the closest classic precedent for a speaking image and technocratic evil. Lewis's essay "The World's Last Night" (1952) is wise on readiness without date-setting.

Tim LaHaye and Jerry B. Jenkins, Left Behind (1995 onward): the best-known dispensational pretribulation novels. They are useful to know as the template many readers expect, and to depart from if your theology differs.

### 10. Guardrails for a faithful AI end-times novel

Never put new words in the mouth of God, Christ, or an angel as if they were Scripture. Quote Scripture accurately, and label invented prophecies as fiction.

Don't name real living people, companies, or nations as the beast, the Antichrist, or the mark. History is full of failed identifications.

Keep the counterfeit trinity (dragon, beast, false prophet) a counterfeit. The Lamb wins by being slain and by His word (Rev 5:9; 19:15).

Make the moral choice clear: worship. Revelation 13-14 is about whom you bow to, not about gadgets.

Keep hope at the center. Revelation ends with a city, a river, a tree, and the face of God (22:1-5). The last word is "Come, Lord Jesus" (22:20).

No date-setting: "of that day and hour no one knows" (Matt 24:36).
