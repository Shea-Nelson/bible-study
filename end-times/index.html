Where every orthodox view agrees

The shared core

Christians disagree about the millennium, the rapture, and how to read Revelation. They agree on far more, and that shared core is confessed in the creeds:

  • Christ will return personally, bodily, and visibly. He "will come in just the same way as you have watched Him go into heaven" (Acts 1:11). Apostles' Creed: he "will come again to judge the living and the dead." Nicene Creed: "he will come again in glory to judge the living and the dead, and his kingdom will have no end."
  • The dead will be raised bodily. "The resurrection of the body" (Apostles'); "we look for the resurrection of the dead" (Nicene); John 5:28-29; 1 Cor 15.
  • There will be a final judgment of all people by Christ (Matt 25:31-46; Acts 17:31; Rev 20:11-15).
  • God will make a new heavens and new earth where He dwells with His people forever, "and the life of the world to come" (Nicene; Isa 65:17; 2 Pet 3:13; Rev 21-22).
  • No one knows the day or hour (Matt 24:36), and evil will be finally defeated (Rev 20:10).

On this site, Shared marks what historic Christians broadly hold, and Debated marks a disputed view or detail. Each view page ends with Solomon's assessment, our humble judgment about which reading the text best supports and why.

How to use this for the novel

Start with the views and pick a framework for your story world. A novel is most coherent when it commits to one view, as Left Behind committed to pretribulation dispensationalism. Then use the timeline for that view as your plot's skeleton, the character cards for your cast, and the AI page to keep the technology faithful to what the text actually says.
