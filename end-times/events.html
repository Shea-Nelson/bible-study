Events

40 events in the story of the end

Listed roughly in the order futurists place them. Amillennial and preterist readers see many as parallel or already fulfilled, so treat the order as a map, not a verdict.

01 · Already Shared

Christ's first coming inaugurates the last days

The New Testament says the "last days" began with Jesus' life, death, resurrection, ascension, and the outpouring of the Spirit. The future is anchored in what God has already done.

Hebrews 1:1-2Acts 2:16-171 Corinthians 10:11Matthew 12:28

How the views read it

Dispensational
Agrees that the church age is the "last days," but sees prophecies about Israel's kingdom as postponed.
Historic premil
The kingdom has been inaugurated and will be consummated in an earthly millennium.
Amillennial
The kingdom has been inaugurated, and the millennium is this present age.
Postmillennial
The kingdom has been inaugurated and will grow until it fills the earth (Dan 2:35).
Preterist reading
The "last days" were mainly the last days of the old covenant era, ending in AD 70.

02 · Signs Debated

Beginning of birth pains

False christs, wars, famines, and earthquakes. Jesus says, "See that you are not alarmed" and calls them "merely the beginning of birth pains."

Matthew 24:4-8Mark 13:5-8Luke 21:8-11

How the views read it

Dispensational
General signs that intensify in the future tribulation; some place them in the first half of the 70th week.
Historic premil
Marks of the whole age that grow more intense before the end.
Amillennial
Marks of the whole age between Christ's two comings.
Postmillennial
Fulfilled in large part before AD 70; such troubles do not stop the gospel's advance.
Preterist reading
Fulfilled before AD 70: false messiahs, the famine under Claudius (Acts 11:28), wars, and earthquakes.

03 · Signs Shared

Persecution and the gospel to all nations

"This gospel of the kingdom shall be proclaimed in the whole world as a witness to all the nations, and then the end will come" (Matt 24:14).

Matthew 24:9-14Mark 13:9-13Revelation 6:9-11

How the views read it

Dispensational
Completed in part by the 144,000 and angelic preaching during the tribulation (Rev 14:6).
Historic premil
Mission to every people must be completed before the end.
Amillennial
The church's present mission; the end waits for it.
Postmillennial
Ensures global conversion of the nations before Christ returns.
Preterist reading
Fulfilled in the apostolic mission to the known world (Col 1:6, 23), then continues.

04 · History Shared

Fall of Jerusalem and the temple (AD 70)

Historical fact: Roman armies under Titus destroyed Jerusalem and the temple in AD 70, as Jesus foretold. Josephus (Jewish War, books 5-6) is the main ancient account. The debate is how much of the Olivet Discourse refers to it.

Matthew 24:1-2Luke 19:41-44Luke 21:20-24

How the views read it

Dispensational
Luke 21:20-24 fulfilled in AD 70; most of Matthew 24 still future.
Historic premil
A partial fulfillment and foreshadowing of the end.
Amillennial
A real fulfillment and a type of the final judgment.
Postmillennial
The great tribulation of Matthew 24:15-28; a central fulfillment.
Preterist reading
The "coming" of the Son of Man in judgment on Jerusalem (Matt 24:30, read with Dan 7:13 imagery).

05 · Signs Debated

The great apostasy

A falling away or rebellion must come before the Day of the Lord.

2 Thessalonians 2:3Matthew 24:10-121 Timothy 4:1-32 Timothy 3:1-5

How the views read it

Dispensational
A future, global departure from the faith; a minority reads apostasia as the rapture ("departure").
Historic premil
A future defection just before the return.
Amillennial
Recurring across the age; peaks just before the end.
Postmillennial
Some place it in the first century (the Jewish revolt or apostasy), others before the end.
Preterist reading
The Jewish revolt against Rome or the first-century apostasy (Gentry).

06 · Signs Debated

The restrainer removed

What restrains the lawless one is "taken out of the way," and then he is revealed.

2 Thessalonians 2:6-7

How the views read it

Dispensational
The Spirit's restraining work through the church is removed at the rapture.
Historic premil
Proposals vary: government, gospel preaching, Michael.
Amillennial
God's providential restraint (often government and law) is lifted near the end.
Postmillennial
Often identified with first-century powers, such as the Roman order or Claudius.
Preterist reading
A first-century restraint, such as Rome or Claudius (Gentry), lifted before Nero's persecution.

07 · Return Shared

Rapture (gathering of the elect)

Living believers are "caught up together with them in the clouds to meet the Lord in the air" (1 Thess 4:17). Every view affirms the event; its timing is the dispute.

1 Thessalonians 4:13-181 Corinthians 15:51-52Matthew 24:31John 14:1-3

How the views read it

Dispensational
Pretribulational: a separate coming for the church before the 70th week.
Historic premil
Posttribulational: at the one Second Coming, the church meets the Lord and returns with Him.
Amillennial
Part of the one Second Coming, together with the general resurrection.
Postmillennial
Part of the one Second Coming at the end of history.
Preterist reading
Future, at the one Second Coming (partial preterists); full preterists spiritualize it, which is not orthodox.

08 · Tribulation Debated

The covenant and the 70th week begin

"He will make a firm covenant with the many for one week" (Dan 9:27).

Daniel 9:27

How the views read it

Dispensational
The Antichrist signs a seven-year treaty with Israel; the tribulation begins.
Historic premil
Many hold to a final seven years; some are less precise.
Amillennial
Usually Christ confirms the covenant; the 70th week is fulfilled or symbolic.
Postmillennial
Christ confirms the new covenant; His death ends sacrifice in the middle of the week.
Preterist reading
Fulfilled in Christ's ministry and the events leading to AD 70.

09 · Tribulation Debated

A rebuilt temple

Many futurists expect a rebuilt Jerusalem temple where sacrifices resume and the lawless one sits. No text explicitly predicts a third temple. The expectation is inferred from 2 Thess 2:4, Rev 11:1-2, and Dan 9:27.

2 Thessalonians 2:4Revelation 11:1-2Ezekiel 40-48Daniel 9:27

How the views read it

Dispensational
A literal tribulation temple and a millennial temple (Ezek 40-48).
Historic premil
Some expect one; others read the "temple" in 2 Thess 2:4 as the church or a figure of speech.
Amillennial
"Temple of God" means the church (1 Cor 3:16) or is figurative, and the lawless one usurps God's place among His people.
Postmillennial
Refers to the Herodian temple before AD 70.
Preterist reading
Fulfilled in the Herodian temple before AD 70.

10 · Tribulation Debated

Abomination of desolation

Jesus said: "when you see the abomination of desolation... standing in the holy place... flee to the mountains" (Matt 24:15-16). The first fulfillment was Antiochus IV in 167 BC.

Daniel 9:27Daniel 11:31Daniel 12:11Matthew 24:15Luke 21:20

How the views read it

Dispensational
Antichrist's desecration of a rebuilt temple at the midpoint of the seven years.
Historic premil
A future desecration by the Antichrist; AD 70 was a foreshadowing.
Amillennial
Fulfilled in AD 70 and may have a final echo.
Postmillennial
Fulfilled in AD 70 (Luke 21:20, Jerusalem surrounded by armies).
Preterist reading
Fulfilled in AD 70: Roman armies or the Zealots' defilement of the temple.

11 · Tribulation Debated

The great tribulation

A distress unequaled "since the beginning of the world until now, nor ever will" (Matt 24:21). The days are cut short for the elect's sake.

Matthew 24:21-22Daniel 12:1Revelation 7:14Revelation 13:5-7

How the views read it

Dispensational
The last 3½ years of the seven-year tribulation; the church is already in heaven.
Historic premil
The final 3½ years (or a final period); the church endures it.
Amillennial
The tribulation of the whole age that peaks just before the return.
Postmillennial
The siege of Jerusalem in AD 66-70.
Preterist reading
The siege of Jerusalem in AD 66-70 (Josephus).

26 · Return Shared

The visible Second Coming of Christ

"Behold, He is coming with the clouds, and every eye will see Him" (Rev 1:7). Jesus "will come in just the same way" as He went (Acts 1:11). Every orthodox view confesses this.

“Behold, He is coming with the clouds, and every eye will see Him”Revelation 1:7 (LSB)
Matthew 24:27-31Acts 1:11Revelation 1:7Revelation 19:11-161 Thessalonians 4:16Titus 2:13

Dispensational
The "second phase": Christ returns with His saints after the tribulation to set up the millennium.
Historic premil
One posttribulational return before the millennium.
Amillennial
One return at the end of the age, followed by the general resurrection and judgment.
Postmillennial
One return after the millennial golden age.
Preterist reading
Partial preterists: a future, bodily return (Acts 1:11). Full preterists deny this, which is not orthodox.

28 · Return Shared

How the views read it

Dispensational
Several stages: the rapture, tribulation saints, the end of the millennium (the wicked).
Historic premil
Two resurrections, of believers before the millennium and of the rest after it (Rev 20:4-6).
Amillennial
One general resurrection at the return (John 5:28-29; Acts 24:15); the "first resurrection" is spiritual.
Postmillennial
One general resurrection at the return.
Preterist reading
Future and bodily (partial preterists).
