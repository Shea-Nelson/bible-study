# End Times Character Cards

*41 cards. "For the novelist" notes are fiction and speculation, not interpretations of prophecy. Scripture quotations are from the LSB.*

## Jesus Christ

**Type:** Divine  
**Also called:** The Lamb; the Rider on the white horse; the Son of Man; the Word of God; the Lion of Judah

**Key texts:** Revelation 1:12-18; Revelation 5:5-14; Revelation 19:11-16; Daniel 7:13-14; Matthew 24:30; Acts 1:11

> "a Lamb standing, as if slain" (Revelation 5:6, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** The center of every end-times text. John sees "one like a son of man" with white hair, eyes "like a flame of fire," and a sword from His mouth (Rev 1:13-16). In heaven He is the Lion of Judah who appears as "a Lamb standing, as if slain," the only one worthy to open the scroll (5:5-6). At the climax He rides a white horse, "Faithful and True," called "The Word of God," with the name "KING OF KINGS, AND LORD OF LORDS" (19:11-16). He also holds "the keys of death and of Hades" (1:18).

**Role in the end times:** Opens the seals and directs history, returns visibly in glory, raises the dead, judges, destroys the beast "with the breath of His mouth" (2 Thess 2:8), marries His bride, and reigns forever.

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* The rider of Rev 19 is the literal Second Coming after the tribulation.
- *Preterist:* Many texts about His "coming" (Matt 24:30) are a judgment-coming on Jerusalem in AD 70. Partial preterists still expect His future bodily return.
- *Historicist:* Christ guides the church through the ages of history mapped by Revelation.
- *Idealist:* The Lamb's victory at the cross is the key to every vision. The rider is His final triumph.

**Solomon's reading:** Every view agrees: He is coming back bodily and visibly, and He wins by His word, not by Satan's methods (Rev 19:15). The Lamb is the true power in Revelation: He conquers by being slain (5:9).

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** In an age of deepfakes, the most faithful scene may be the simplest one. His return needs no screen and cannot be faked (Matt 24:26-27: like lightning, seen from east to west). A novelist can contrast every simulated "messiah" with the One whose glory overwhelms every feed at once. Do not put invented words in His mouth beyond Scripture.

## God the Father on the throne

**Type:** Divine  
**Also called:** The One who sits on the throne; the Ancient of Days

**Key texts:** Revelation 4:2-11; Daniel 7:9-10; Revelation 20:11; Revelation 21:5; Isaiah 6:1-3

> "a throne was standing in heaven, and One sitting on the throne" (Revelation 4:2, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** "A throne was standing in heaven, and One sitting on the throne," like jasper and sardius, with a rainbow "like an emerald" around it (Rev 4:2-3). Daniel calls Him the Ancient of Days, with white garments and a throne of fire (Dan 7:9). He holds the sealed scroll (Rev 5:1), and from the great white throne "earth and heaven fled away" (20:11). He says, "Behold, I am making all things new" (21:5).

**Role in the end times:** The sovereign Creator whose throne is the control room of the whole book (Rev 4 comes before any judgment). He gives the kingdom to the Son and judges the world.

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* Literal heavenly throne-room scenes before future judgments.
- *Preterist:* The throne stands over history, including the events of AD 70.
- *Historicist:* God's providence over church history.
- *Idealist:* The unseen reality behind every age: God reigns now.

**Solomon's reading:** Revelation 4-5 is the hinge. Before any seal is broken, John is shown that the throne is occupied. Every view agrees on this.

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** Characters overwhelmed by systems they cannot control can be shown the throne room, a reminder that no algorithm sits on the throne. Avoid picturing God visually beyond what Scripture gives (light, jasper, rainbow, fire).

## The Holy Spirit (the seven Spirits)

**Type:** Divine  
**Also called:** The seven Spirits of God; the Spirit of prophecy

**Key texts:** Revelation 1:4; Revelation 4:5; Revelation 5:6; Revelation 22:17; Zechariah 4:2-10; 2 Thessalonians 2:6-7

> "seven eyes, which are the seven Spirits of God, sent out into all the earth" (Revelation 5:6, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** "The seven Spirits who are before His throne" (Rev 1:4) appear as "seven lamps of fire" (4:5) and as the Lamb's "seven horns and seven eyes, which are the seven Spirits of God, sent out into all the earth" (5:6). At the close, "the Spirit and the bride say, 'Come'" (22:17).

**Role in the end times:** Speaks to the churches ("hear what the Spirit says," Rev 2-3), empowers witness, sees all the earth, and in some views is the restrainer of 2 Thess 2.

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* The seven Spirits are the Holy Spirit. Many dispensationalists see Him as the restrainer, removed with the church at the rapture (He stays present, but His restraining role ends).
- *Preterist:* The Spirit present to the first-century churches.
- *Historicist:* The Spirit preserving the true church through history.
- *Idealist:* The fullness of the Spirit (seven = completeness) at work in every age.

**Solomon's reading:** Most commentators (Bauckham, Beale) read "seven Spirits" as the one Holy Spirit in His fullness, drawing on Zechariah 4. The "seven eyes" sent into all the earth are God's own omniscient presence.

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** Revelation has a true all-seeing presence, the Spirit's "seven eyes... sent out into all the earth." A fictional surveillance AI is a counterfeit omniscience. The contrast between eyes that watch to control and eyes that see to save could carry your theme.

## The Antichrist / man of lawlessness

**Type:** Human (prophetic)  
**Also called:** Man of sin; son of destruction; the lawless one

**Key texts:** 2 Thessalonians 2:3-12; 1 John 2:18-22; 1 John 4:3; 2 John 7; Daniel 7:8; Daniel 7:24-26; Daniel 11:36-39

> "the man of lawlessness is revealed, the son of destruction" (2 Thessalonians 2:3, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** Paul's "man of lawlessness... the son of destruction" opposes and exalts himself "above every so-called god or object of worship," takes his seat "in the sanctuary of God, exhibiting himself as being God" (2 Thess 2:3-4). His coming is "in accord with the working of Satan, with all power and signs and false wonders" (2:9), and the Lord Jesus will "slay" him "with the breath of His mouth" (2:8). John says "antichrist is coming" and "even now many antichrists have appeared" (1 John 2:18).

**Role in the end times:** The final human champion of rebellion, a self-deifying ruler revealed after the apostasy and the removal of the restrainer, and destroyed at Christ's appearing.

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* One future world ruler: the little horn, the beast from the sea, and the man of lawlessness are the same person.
- *Preterist:* A first-century figure, Nero, or a Jewish revolutionary leader (Gentry favors Nero for the beast; views of 2 Thess 2 vary).
- *Historicist:* The papacy as an institution (the Reformers; Westminster Confession 25.6, original 1646 text).
- *Idealist:* The spirit of antichrist in every age, culminating in a final person (Riddlebarger, The Man of Sin, 2006).

**Solomon's reading:** The text supports both: a present antichristian spirit (1 John) and a final, personal lawless one destroyed at Christ's return (2 Thess 2:8 ties his end to the parousia). Identifying him with a living person has failed every time.

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** A self-deifying human leader who uses AI for "signs and false wonders" fits the text better than an AI that is itself the Antichrist. Scripture calls him a "man" (anthrōpos). The lie he sells is old: "you will be like God" (Gen 3:5). Do not name any real person.

## The beast from the sea

**Type:** Beast  
**Also called:** The first beast; the scarlet beast

**Key texts:** Revelation 13:1-10; Revelation 17:3; Revelation 17:8-14; Revelation 19:19-20; Daniel 7:2-8

> "Who is like the beast, and who is able to wage war with him?" (Revelation 13:4, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** "A beast coming up out of the sea, having ten horns and seven heads, and on his horns were ten diadems, and on his heads were blasphemous names" (Rev 13:1). He is like a leopard, with feet like a bear and a mouth like a lion, combining Daniel's four beasts. "The dragon gave him his power and his throne and great authority" (13:2). One head seemed "slain fatally, and his fatal wound was healed," and "the whole earth marveled" (13:3). He blasphemes for 42 months and wars against the saints.

**Role in the end times:** The political arm of the satanic trinity: a world empire and its ruler who demands worship. He ends thrown alive into the lake of fire (19:20).

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* A future revived empire (often a revived Rome) and its ruler, the Antichrist. The healed wound may be a literal assassination and recovery.
- *Preterist:* Rome and Nero. The healed wound is the Nero redivivus legend or Rome's recovery after the civil wars of AD 68-69. 666 = Nero Caesar.
- *Historicist:* Papal Rome.
- *Idealist:* Persecuting state power in every age (Babylon, Rome, modern totalitarian regimes), heading to a final form.

**Solomon's reading:** The sea is the chaos-symbol of Daniel 7 and the beast is Daniel's beasts combined, so first-century Rome was its embodiment for John's readers (17:9). Its features recur in history and point to a final climax. The parody is the point: the healed wound mimics the Lamb "as if slain."

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** A global governance platform that "recovers" after a crash or collapse and is hailed as indispensable ("Who is like the beast, and who is able to wage war with him?" 13:4). Mark it clearly as fiction and avoid real nations or companies.

## The beast from the earth (the false prophet)

**Type:** Beast  
**Also called:** The false prophet; the second beast

**Key texts:** Revelation 13:11-17; Revelation 16:13; Revelation 19:20; Revelation 20:10; Matthew 24:24

> "he had two horns like a lamb and he was speaking as a dragon" (Revelation 13:11, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** "Another beast coming up out of the earth, and he had two horns like a lamb and he was speaking as a dragon" (Rev 13:11). He exercises the first beast's authority, makes the earth worship it, "does great signs, so that he even makes fire come down out of heaven" (13:13), deceives "those who dwell on the earth," commands an image, gives it breath, and enforces the mark (13:14-17). Later he is called "the false prophet" (16:13; 19:20).

**Role in the end times:** The propaganda and religious arm. He makes worship of the beast look reasonable, miraculous, and necessary, and he controls the economy.

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* A future religious leader, partner of the Antichrist.
- *Preterist:* The imperial cult's priesthood in Asia Minor (the provincial koinon), or the Jewish leaders who cooperated with Rome.
- *Historicist:* The papal hierarchy, or false religion in general.
- *Idealist:* Every religion, ideology, or propaganda system that serves the state's idolatry.

**Solomon's reading:** He looks like a lamb but speaks like a dragon. The danger is a gentle-seeming persuader. In John's setting, the imperial cult's priests and city elites promoted emperor worship, and the pattern recurs.

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** A friendly, lamb-like AI interface that speaks with the dragon's voice: persuasive, helpful, miraculous-seeming, always pointing worship to the system. The "fire from heaven" could be shown as spectacle that mimics Elijah (1 Kgs 18:38), but keep the text's point: signs prove nothing by themselves (Deut 13:1-3).

## The image of the beast

**Type:** Object / power  
**Also called:** The speaking image

**Key texts:** Revelation 13:14-15; Revelation 14:9-11; Revelation 15:2; Revelation 20:4; Daniel 3:1-7; Habakkuk 2:18-19

> "so that the image of the beast would even speak" (Revelation 13:15, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** The earth-beast tells the world "to make an image to the beast," and "it was given to him to give breath to the image of the beast, so that the image of the beast would even speak and cause as many as do not worship the image of the beast to be killed" (Rev 13:14-15). The word is eikōn (G1504), the same word for humanity made "in the image" of God in the Greek Old Testament (Gen 1:26) and for Christ, "the image of the invisible God" (Col 1:15).

**Role in the end times:** A man-made object of worship that appears alive and enforces loyalty on pain of death. The saints are those who refuse it (15:2; 20:4).

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* A literal image: a statue, hologram, robot, or AI. Many popular writers suggest technology. The text says only that it is given breath and speaks.
- *Preterist:* Statues of the emperor in imperial-cult temples. Ancient writers report "talking" statues and staged oracles; Lucian's Alexander the False Prophet describes a fake speaking-serpent oracle.
- *Historicist:* Papal or ecclesiastical images, or an institution made in the beast's likeness.
- *Idealist:* Any idol a culture makes in the likeness of its own power and then obeys.

**Solomon's reading:** Note the passives: "it was given" to him to give it breath. Even this marvel works only under God's permission. The prophets mocked idols that have "no breath at all inside" (Hab 2:19). The final idol counterfeits life itself. Whether its breath is technology, deception, or demonic power, the text does not say.

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** This is the most natural AI angle in Revelation. An AI "image" made by human hands, given a voice, that people are pressured to honor. Stay faithful to the text: it is an image of the beast, made at the false prophet's command, and its "breath" is permitted. In the story, it should be a counterfeit of God's image-bearers, not a new kind of person. The book of Daniel gives a model: three young men who would not bow to an image under threat of death (Dan 3).

## The dragon (Satan, the devil)

**Type:** Spiritual (fallen)  
**Also called:** The serpent of old; the accuser; the deceiver

**Key texts:** Revelation 12:3-17; Revelation 13:2-4; Revelation 20:1-3; Revelation 20:7-10; Genesis 3:1-15; Job 1:6-12; Isaiah 27:1

> "the serpent of old who is called the devil and Satan, who deceives the whole world" (Revelation 12:9, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** "A great red dragon having seven heads and ten horns" whose tail sweeps "a third of the stars of heaven" (Rev 12:3-4). He waits to devour the male child, makes war in heaven and loses, and is thrown down: "the serpent of old who is called the devil and Satan, who deceives the whole world" (12:9). He gives his throne to the beast, is bound for a thousand years, is released, deceives the nations, and is thrown into the lake of fire forever (20:10).

**Role in the end times:** Head of the counterfeit trinity (dragon, beast, false prophet). He is the accuser, the deceiver, and the persecutor of the woman and "the rest of her seed."

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* A personal devil whose future expulsion and binding follow a set timetable.
- *Preterist:* A personal devil defeated at the cross and acting through Rome and apostate Jerusalem.
- *Historicist:* Satan acting through pagan and then papal Rome.
- *Idealist:* Satan's war against Christ and His people in every age.

**Solomon's reading:** Scripture treats Satan as a real, personal, created being (Job 1; Matt 4), powerful but defeated and on a leash. Revelation 12:11 shows how the saints overcome him: "because of the blood of the Lamb and because of the word of their witness."

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** Satan's oldest method is deception and accusation (12:9-10). An AI that floods the world with accusations, files, and exposures could be a picture of the accuser, but keep the spiritual being distinct from any machine.

## The four horsemen

**Type:** Spiritual (agents of judgment)  
**Also called:** White, red, black, and pale horses

**Key texts:** Revelation 6:1-8; Zechariah 1:8-11; Zechariah 6:1-8; Ezekiel 14:21

> "he went out overcoming and to overcome" (Revelation 6:2, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** When the Lamb opens the first four seals, a living creature says "Come" each time. A white horse and rider with a bow "went out overcoming and to overcome." A red horse is given power to "take peace from the earth." A black horse has a rider with scales and the price of wheat inflated ("one choinix of wheat for one denarius"). A pale horse's rider is named Death, "and Hades was following with him," killing a fourth of the earth by sword, famine, pestilence, and beasts (Rev 6:1-8).

**Role in the end times:** Agents of conquest, war, economic famine, and death, released only by the Lamb, within limits ("a fourth"; "do not harm the oil and the wine").

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* The first judgments of the future tribulation. The white rider is often the Antichrist's false peace.
- *Preterist:* Judgments of the Jewish war against Rome.
- *Historicist:* Phases of Roman imperial history.
- *Idealist:* Recurring conditions of the whole age under Christ's sovereign control. Some (for example, Irenaeus, and some modern readers) see the white rider as Christ or the gospel.

**Solomon's reading:** The four echo Zechariah's horses and Ezekiel's "four severe judgments" (Ezek 14:21). The white rider is debated. Its bow and conquest resemble a parody of Christ (Rev 19) more than Christ Himself, though good interpreters differ. The horsemen are released by the Lamb, so even disaster is under His hand.

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** The black horse rider's scales and wheat priced at a day's wage fit a world where algorithmic pricing creates scarcity while luxuries are spared ("do not harm the oil and the wine"). Keep the riders as agents of judgment under the Lamb, not as random chaos.

## The two witnesses

**Type:** Human (prophetic)  
**Also called:** Two olive trees; two lampstands

**Key texts:** Revelation 11:3-13; Zechariah 4:1-14; Malachi 4:5; Deuteronomy 19:15; Revelation 1:20

> "they will prophesy for 1,260 days, clothed in sackcloth" (Revelation 11:3, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** "I will give authority to my two witnesses, and they will prophesy for 1,260 days, clothed in sackcloth. These are the two olive trees and the two lampstands that stand before the Lord of the earth" (Rev 11:3-4). Fire comes from their mouths, they shut the sky like Elijah and turn water to blood like Moses. When their testimony is finished, the beast from the abyss kills them. Their bodies lie "in the street of the great city which spiritually is called Sodom and Egypt, where also their Lord was crucified" (11:8). The world celebrates and sends gifts, but after 3½ days "the breath of life from God" enters them and they ascend in a cloud (11:11-12).

**Role in the end times:** Faithful prophetic witness during the 1,260 days. They are killed and publicly vindicated.

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* Two individual prophets in a future Jerusalem: Moses and Elijah, or Enoch and Elijah (an early tradition in Hippolytus and Tertullian), or two unknown people.
- *Preterist:* Christian witnesses, or a pair such as Peter and Paul, before AD 70.
- *Historicist:* Witnesses to truth through the medieval period, such as the Waldensians.
- *Idealist:* The witnessing church. Lampstands are churches (1:20), and two witnesses make valid testimony (Deut 19:15).

**Solomon's reading:** The symbols (lampstands, olive trees, Moses-and-Elijah powers) point strongly to the church's prophetic witness pictured in two figures. Their story follows their Lord's: witness, death, resurrection, ascension. Futurists rightly note the detail and specificity.

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** People from every nation "will look at their dead bodies for three and a half days" (11:9). That instantly global viewing has made modern readers think of broadcast media. In a novel, two witnesses whose deaths are livestreamed and celebrated, then whose rising cannot be explained away, could stay close to the text.

## Michael the archangel

**Type:** Angelic

**Key texts:** Daniel 10:13; Daniel 10:21; Daniel 12:1; Jude 9; Revelation 12:7-9; 1 Thessalonians 4:16

> "Michael, the great prince who stands guard over the sons of your people, will stand" (Daniel 12:1, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** "Michael, one of the chief princes" helps the angel in Daniel against "the prince of the kingdom of Persia" (Dan 10:13). At the time of the end, "Michael, the great prince who stands guard over the sons of your people, will stand" (12:1). Jude calls him "the archangel" who said to the devil, "The Lord rebuke you!" (Jude 9). In Revelation he and his angels throw the dragon out of heaven (Rev 12:7-9).

**Role in the end times:** Guardian of God's people, warrior against Satan, and a figure linked with the final distress and resurrection (Dan 12:1-2).

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* He stands up at the midpoint of the tribulation. Some dispensationalists suggest he is the restrainer.
- *Preterist:* His war in heaven reflects the victory of the cross in the first century.
- *Historicist:* Sometimes read as a symbol of Christ (some Reformers and Adventist writers).
- *Idealist:* He pictures heaven's warfare on behalf of the church, won through the Lamb.

**Solomon's reading:** Michael is a created angel, the highest named. Jude shows he does not act on his own authority ("The Lord rebuke you"). His victory in Revelation 12 comes "because of the blood of the Lamb" (12:11).

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** Daniel 10 shows unseen "princes" behind nations. A thriller could hint at spiritual warfare behind geopolitical AI races, but keep angels as servants of God, not superheroes, and do not invent revelations about them.

## Gabriel

**Type:** Angelic

**Key texts:** Daniel 8:15-27; Daniel 9:20-27; Luke 1:19; Luke 1:26-38

> "I am Gabriel, who stands before God" (Luke 1:19, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** The interpreting angel of Daniel 8 and 9. He explains the ram and goat and brings the prophecy of the seventy weeks (Dan 9:21-27). In Luke he says, "I am Gabriel, who stands before God" (Luke 1:19), and announces the births of John and Jesus.

**Role in the end times:** God's messenger of prophetic timetables and of the Messiah's coming. He links Daniel's end-time visions to the Gospels.

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* He reveals the 70 weeks, including a future 70th week.
- *Preterist:* He reveals the 70 weeks, fulfilled in Christ and AD 70.
- *Historicist:* He reveals a timeline that runs through history.
- *Idealist:* He shows that God's purposes keep His timetable.

**Solomon's reading:** Gabriel connects Daniel's "seventy weeks... until Messiah the Prince" with the annunciation. The same messenger frames the countdown and announces its goal.

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** A messenger who "stands before God" contrasts with machine oracles that answer anything. Gabriel brings understanding (Dan 9:22) and sometimes silence (Luke 1:20). Do not give him extra-biblical prophecies as if they were real.

## The seven angels (trumpets and bowls)

**Type:** Angelic

**Key texts:** Revelation 8:2; Revelation 8:6-13; Revelation 15:1; Revelation 15:6-8; Revelation 16:1-21; Revelation 17:1; Revelation 21:9

> "the seven angels who stand before God" (Revelation 8:2, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** "The seven angels who stand before God, and seven trumpets were given to them" (Rev 8:2). Seven angels later come out of the heavenly sanctuary with the seven last plagues and receive "seven golden bowls full of the wrath of God" (15:7). One of the bowl angels shows John both the harlot (17:1) and the bride (21:9).

**Role in the end times:** Executors of God's judgments in measured, sevenfold order.

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* Literal angels carrying out consecutive future judgments.
- *Preterist:* Agents of judgment on Jerusalem.
- *Historicist:* Agents of judgment in successive historical periods.
- *Idealist:* Heaven's ordering of judgment across the age.

**Solomon's reading:** The same kind of angel shows John both Babylon and the New Jerusalem (17:1; 21:9). The book is a tale of two cities.

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** Judgment in Revelation is ordered, limited, and announced. It is never random. A story can let disasters feel chaotic on earth while hinting at the calm, liturgical order of heaven behind them.

## The four living creatures

**Type:** Angelic

**Key texts:** Revelation 4:6-9; Revelation 5:8-14; Revelation 6:1-7; Revelation 15:7; Ezekiel 1:4-14; Ezekiel 10:20; Isaiah 6:1-3

> "day and night they do not cease to say" (Revelation 4:8, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** Around the throne are "four living creatures full of eyes in front and behind." The first is like a lion, the second like a calf, the third has "a face like that of a man," and the fourth is "like a flying eagle." Each has six wings, and "day and night they do not cease to say, 'Holy, holy, holy is the Lord God, the Almighty, who was and who is and who is to come'" (Rev 4:6-8). They call out "Come" to the horsemen and hand the bowls to the angels.

**Role in the end times:** The highest order of throne-angels (cherubim and seraphim combined). They lead worship and launch the judgments.

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* Literal heavenly beings.
- *Preterist:* Literal heavenly beings.
- *Historicist:* Sometimes read as the four Gospels or as Christian ministers.
- *Idealist:* They represent all creation (wild, domestic, human, bird) worshiping God. Irenaeus linked the four faces to the four Gospels (Against Heresies 3.11.8).

**Solomon's reading:** They are real angelic beings drawn from Ezekiel's cherubim and Isaiah's seraphim. Their many eyes speak of vigilance and knowledge in God's service.

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** Beings "full of eyes" are the Bible's true picture of total watchfulness, and they use it all for worship. A novel can quietly set them against a surveillance network that has all eyes and no worship.

## The twenty-four elders

**Type:** Angelic or human (debated)

**Key texts:** Revelation 4:4; Revelation 4:10-11; Revelation 5:5; Revelation 5:8-10; Revelation 7:13-14; Revelation 11:16-18; Revelation 19:4

> "will cast their crowns before the throne" (Revelation 4:10, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** "Around the throne were twenty-four thrones, and upon those thrones I saw twenty-four elders sitting, clothed in white garments, and golden crowns on their heads" (Rev 4:4). They fall down and "cast their crowns before the throne" (4:10), hold harps and "golden bowls full of incense, which are the prayers of the saints" (5:8), and one of them comforts John (5:5).

**Role in the end times:** A heavenly council that worships, interprets, and offers the saints' prayers.

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* Many dispensationalists: the raptured, rewarded church in heaven before the tribulation.
- *Preterist:* Representatives of the people of God, or an angelic council.
- *Historicist:* Representatives of the church.
- *Idealist:* The whole people of God, old and new covenant (12 tribes + 12 apostles, compare 21:12-14). Others: an angelic priestly council (compare 1 Chr 24's 24 priestly divisions).

**Solomon's reading:** The twelve-plus-twelve pattern of the New Jerusalem (21:12-14) makes the representative view attractive. The angelic view has real support too. The text does not settle it, so hold it loosely.

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** The elders cast down their crowns, the opposite of a world grasping for power. A character's turning point could echo this gesture.

## The 144,000

**Type:** Human (corporate)

**Key texts:** Revelation 7:1-8; Revelation 14:1-5; Ezekiel 9:4; Revelation 9:4

> "no lie was found in their mouth" (Revelation 14:5, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** "I heard the number of those having been sealed, 144,000 sealed from every tribe of the sons of Israel" (Rev 7:4): 12,000 from each of twelve tribes, with Dan omitted and Joseph and Levi included. They have the Lamb's name and the Father's name on their foreheads, follow the Lamb "wherever He goes," and "no lie was found in their mouth" (14:1-5).

**Role in the end times:** God's sealed servants, protected from the demonic locusts (9:4) and standing with the Lamb on Mount Zion.

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* Literal Jewish believers, 12,000 per tribe, often evangelists during the tribulation.
- *Preterist:* Jewish Christians before AD 70, the first fruits of the church.
- *Historicist:* The true church in the period of persecution.
- *Idealist:* The whole church as the true Israel, numbered like a military census (12 x 12 x 1,000). John hears the number and then sees "a great multitude" (7:9), as he heard of a Lion and saw a Lamb (5:5-6).

**Solomon's reading:** The hear/see pattern, the irregular tribe list, and 14:4's "first fruits" incline many (Bauckham, Beale) to see the whole redeemed people. Dispensationalists rightly note the explicit tribal language. This one is honestly debated.

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** Their defining marks are the Lamb's name on the forehead and truthfulness ("no lie was found in their mouth"). In an age of synthetic lies, a remnant known for refusing to lie, even to bots, could be a fitting fictional echo.

## The great multitude

**Type:** Human (corporate)

**Key texts:** Revelation 7:9-17; Revelation 19:1-8; Genesis 15:5; Genesis 22:17

> "a great multitude which no one could count" (Revelation 7:9, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** "A great multitude which no one could count, from every nation and all tribes and peoples and tongues, standing before the throne and before the Lamb, clothed in white robes, and palm branches were in their hands" (Rev 7:9). "These are the ones who come out of the great tribulation, and they washed their robes and made them white in the blood of the Lamb" (7:14). They will hunger and thirst no more, and "God will wipe every tear from their eyes" (7:17).

**Role in the end times:** The redeemed from all nations, the fulfillment of God's promise to Abraham. They sing salvation to God and to the Lamb.

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* Gentiles saved during the future tribulation and martyred.
- *Preterist:* The church that came through first-century persecution.
- *Historicist:* The church triumphant through history.
- *Idealist:* All the redeemed who have come through the tribulation of this age.

**Solomon's reading:** "Come out of the great tribulation" uses the definite article. Whatever its timing, the multitude is the global church saved by the Lamb's blood.

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** Every language is present (7:9). A world of instant machine translation could be shown as a dim, broken echo of this final unity, which happens without erasing nations or tongues.

## The woman clothed with the sun

**Type:** Symbolic figure

**Key texts:** Revelation 12:1-6; Revelation 12:13-17; Genesis 37:9-10; Genesis 3:15; Isaiah 66:7-9; Micah 4:10

> "a woman clothed with the sun, and the moon under her feet" (Revelation 12:1, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** "A great sign appeared in heaven: a woman clothed with the sun, and the moon under her feet, and on her head a crown of twelve stars" (Rev 12:1). She gives birth to the male child, flees into the wilderness to "a place prepared by God" for 1,260 days (12:6), is given "the two wings of the great eagle," and the earth swallows the dragon's flood (12:14-16).

**Role in the end times:** The messianic community, Israel and then the church, that brings forth Messiah and is preserved in the wilderness while the dragon wars "with the rest of her seed" (12:17).

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* National Israel (sun, moon, and twelve stars from Joseph's dream, Gen 37:9), protected in the tribulation, perhaps at Petra.
- *Preterist:* Faithful Israel and the church fleeing Jerusalem before AD 70.
- *Historicist:* The true church in the wilderness through the medieval period.
- *Idealist:* The one people of God across both covenants. Catholic interpreters also see a secondary reference to Mary.

**Solomon's reading:** Genesis 37:9 makes the Israel background certain. Her later life (wilderness protection, "the rest of her seed, who keep the commandments of God and have the witness of Jesus") shows the people of God continuing into the church. Mary is the individual through whom the Messiah came, but the sign is corporate.

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** "A place prepared by God" in the wilderness: off-grid protection and provision outside the system. It is a resonant image for a community that cannot buy or sell, but don't turn it into a survivalist manual.

## The male child

**Type:** Divine (Messiah)

**Key texts:** Revelation 12:5; Psalm 2:7-9; Revelation 19:15; Isaiah 7:14; Isaiah 9:6-7

> "who is to rule all the nations with a rod of iron" (Revelation 12:5, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** "She gave birth to a son, a male child, who is to rule all the nations with a rod of iron; and her child was caught up to God and to His throne" (Rev 12:5).

**Role in the end times:** Jesus Christ: His birth, the dragon's attempt to destroy Him (compare Herod, Matt 2), and His ascension compressed into one verse.

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* Christ, though a few dispensationalists see the raptured church (because of "caught up," harpazō).
- *Preterist:* Christ.
- *Historicist:* Christ, or in some older historicists, Constantine's Christian empire.
- *Idealist:* Christ.

**Solomon's reading:** "Rule all the nations with a rod of iron" quotes Psalm 2:9, a messianic psalm Revelation applies to Jesus (19:15). It is clearly Christ, from incarnation to ascension.

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** Revelation skips straight from birth to ascension. The cross is assumed and the victory is already won. A story set in the dragon's "short time" (12:12) can carry that backdrop: the real war was decided before the plot begins.

## The harlot Babylon

**Type:** Symbolic figure (city)  
**Also called:** Babylon the Great; the great city; the mother of harlots

**Key texts:** Revelation 14:8; Revelation 17:1-18; Revelation 18:1-24; Jeremiah 51:6-9; Isaiah 47:1-15; 1 Peter 5:13

> "Come out of her, my people" (Revelation 18:4, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** "The great harlot who sits on many waters, with whom the kings of the earth committed sexual immorality" (Rev 17:1-2). She rides the scarlet beast, is dressed in purple, scarlet, gold, and pearls, holds a gold cup "full of abominations," and has on her forehead "BABYLON THE GREAT, THE MOTHER OF HARLOTS AND OF THE ABOMINATIONS OF THE EARTH" (17:3-5). She is "drunk with the blood of the saints" (17:6). In Revelation 18, her merchants grow rich "by the power of her sensuality," and her cargo list ends with "human beings and human lives" (18:13). "All the nations were deceived by your sorcery" (18:23). The beast and ten horns turn on her and burn her (17:16).

**Role in the end times:** The seductive world-city: economic, cultural, and religious. She intoxicates the nations, persecutes the saints, and falls "in one hour." God's people are told, "Come out of her, my people" (18:4).

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* A future rebuilt Babylon on the Euphrates, or a global religious and commercial system, possibly centered in a specific city.
- *Preterist:* Imperial Rome (seven mountains, 17:9; 1 Pet 5:13) or apostate Jerusalem (Gentry; 11:8 "where also their Lord was crucified").
- *Historicist:* Papal Rome.
- *Idealist:* The world system in every age that seduces people through wealth, pleasure, and power.

**Solomon's reading:** "Seven mountains" and "the great city, which has a kingdom over the kings of the earth" (17:18) fit first-century Rome very well. As "Babylon" she is also a recurring type: every proud civilization that trades in human souls. The call "come out of her" applies to every generation.

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** Revelation 18 is the Bible's strongest economic critique (see Bauckham, "The Economic Critique of Rome in Revelation 18," in The Climax of Prophecy). An attention economy that sells "human lives," and a world "deceived by your sorcery" (pharmakeia, which means drugs and enchantment), give a novelist rich material. Note that the beast destroys her (17:16). The system devours its own luxury culture.

## The locusts from the abyss

**Type:** Demonic

**Key texts:** Revelation 9:1-11; Joel 1:4-7; Joel 2:1-11; Exodus 10:12-15

> "men will seek death and will never find it" (Revelation 9:6, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** At the fifth trumpet, smoke rises from the abyss and "out of the smoke came locusts upon the earth" (Rev 9:3). They harm only "the men who do not have the seal of God on their foreheads" and torment for five months without killing. People "will seek death and will never find it." They are "like horses prepared for battle," with crowns like gold, faces like men, hair like women, teeth like lions, iron breastplates, wings that sound "like the sound of chariots," and scorpion tails (9:4-10).

**Role in the end times:** Demonic tormentors released under God's limits, an intensified echo of Exodus's eighth plague and Joel's locust army.

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* Literal demonic beings. Hal Lindsey (The Late Great Planet Earth, 1970) famously suggested attack helicopters. Most futurist scholars, Walvoord among them, treat them as demons.
- *Preterist:* The Roman armies or Zealot factions tormenting Judea during the five months of the siege (some note the summer of AD 66 or 70).
- *Historicist:* The Muslim Arab invasions (a classic Protestant historicist reading).
- *Idealist:* Demonic powers of deception and torment that afflict unbelievers in every age.

**Solomon's reading:** The text calls them locusts from the abyss under a king who is "the angel of the abyss." They are demonic in origin. Their "like... like... like" descriptions show John reaching for comparisons. Reading in modern machinery imposes our technology on his vision, though a novelist is free to imagine.

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** Swarming autonomous drones that torment but are not allowed to kill, and that spare those with God's seal, would be a dramatic image. Be honest in an author's note: this is fiction layered on a vision of demonic powers. It is not a decoding of the text.

## Abaddon / Apollyon

**Type:** Demonic

**Key texts:** Revelation 9:11; Job 26:6; Job 28:22; Proverbs 15:11; Psalm 88:11

> "the angel of the abyss" (Revelation 9:11, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** "They have as king over them, the angel of the abyss. His name in Hebrew is Abaddon, and in the Greek he has the name Apollyon" (Rev 9:11). In the Old Testament, Abaddon ("destruction") is a poetic name for the realm of the dead alongside Sheol (Job 26:6; Prov 15:11).

**Role in the end times:** King of the locust horde, ruler from the abyss. He is destruction personified.

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* A powerful fallen angel, or Satan himself.
- *Preterist:* Sometimes read as an allusion to Apollo, the god Domitian or Nero associated themselves with, so a dig at imperial self-deification.
- *Historicist:* Linked with the leaders of the invading powers.
- *Idealist:* The destroying power of the demonic realm.

**Solomon's reading:** The Greek name Apollyon may well be a wordplay on Apollo, a god emperors claimed as patron. Many commentators (for example, Aune and Beale) note it. Whether he is Satan or a subordinate is uncertain, but he is clearly evil, and he still answers to God's limits.

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** A destroyer whose name puns on an emperor's god. A novelist could have a project or system named after a god of light and reason that turns out to be a destroyer. That stays faithful to the irony of the text without claiming any real product fulfills it.

## The four angels bound at the Euphrates

**Type:** Spiritual (bound)

**Key texts:** Revelation 9:13-15; Revelation 7:1-3; Revelation 16:12; Genesis 15:18

> "Release the four angels who have been bound at the great river Euphrates" (Revelation 9:14, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** At the sixth trumpet a voice from the golden altar says, "Release the four angels who have been bound at the great river Euphrates." They "had been prepared for the hour and day and month and year, so that they would kill a third of mankind" (Rev 9:14-15).

**Role in the end times:** Bound fallen powers released at the exact moment God appoints, unleashing the vast cavalry.

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* Literal fallen angels released at a future hour, leading armies from the east.
- *Preterist:* The Roman legions stationed by the Euphrates that came against Judea.
- *Historicist:* The Turkish (Ottoman) invasions.
- *Idealist:* God's restraint on destructive powers, lifted in judgment.

**Solomon's reading:** Being "bound" marks them as evil spirits, since holy angels are not chained. Their release is timed to "the hour and day and month and year": God's sovereignty is precise even over unleashed evil. The Euphrates was Israel's ideal northern border (Gen 15:18) and the line beyond which invaders came.

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** Restraints released by a single command from the altar. Stories about AI safety "guardrails" being removed could echo this, so long as the story keeps God, not the engineers, as the One who times the hour.

## The 200 million horsemen

**Type:** Demonic / army

**Key texts:** Revelation 9:16-19; Revelation 16:12

> "the number of the armies of the horsemen was two hundred million" (Revelation 9:16, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** "The number of the armies of the horsemen was two hundred million; I heard the number of them" (Rev 9:16). The riders have breastplates of fire, hyacinth, and brimstone. The horses have heads like lions, and from their mouths come "fire and smoke and brimstone" that kill a third of mankind. "Their tails are like serpents, having heads" (9:17-19).

**Role in the end times:** The second woe: a massive, monstrous cavalry of judgment. Even so, the survivors "did not repent" (9:20-21).

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* A literal army: some popular writers suggest a Chinese army, but scholarly futurists more often see demonic hordes.
- *Preterist:* Roman or Parthian forces.
- *Historicist:* The Ottoman Turks and their cannon (fire and smoke).
- *Idealist:* Hellish forces of destruction released in judgment.

**Solomon's reading:** The number is literally double myriads of myriads (dismyriades myriadōn), a figure of incalculable size, like the angels' "myriads of myriads" in 5:11. The monstrous features mark it as demonic. The key verse is 9:20: judgment alone does not produce repentance.

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** Their power is "in their mouths and in their tails." A novelist could explore weapons of speech as well as fire. The theological note to keep: people "did not repent of the works of their hands, so as not to worship demons, and the idols... which can neither see nor hear nor walk" (9:20).

## Gog and Magog

**Type:** Human (nations)

**Key texts:** Ezekiel 38:1-23; Ezekiel 39:1-29; Revelation 20:7-10; Genesis 10:2

> "so that the nations may know Me" (Ezekiel 38:16, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** "Son of man, set your face toward Gog of the land of Magog, the chief prince of Meshech and Tubal" (Ezek 38:2). Gog comes "in the last years" against Israel, "living securely," from "the remote parts of the north... a great assembly and a numerous military force" (38:8, 15). God destroys the host with earthquake, hail, fire, and brimstone so "that the nations may know Me" (38:16). In Revelation, after the thousand years, Satan gathers "the nations which are in the four corners of the earth, Gog and Magog" against "the camp of the saints and the beloved city," and fire from heaven devours them (Rev 20:8-9).

**Role in the end times:** The last great coalition against God's people, defeated directly by God.

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* Many dispensationalists: Ezekiel's invasion is future (before or during the tribulation, often led by Russia via "Rosh"), and Revelation 20 is a later post-millennial revolt.
- *Preterist:* Ezekiel's prophecy fulfilled in the past (some point to the Seleucids, or James Jordan to the events of Esther). Revelation 20 is future.
- *Historicist:* Various historical enemies (Turks, Saracens).
- *Idealist:* Every final assault of the world on the church. John uses Ezekiel's language for the end of the age (and in 19:17-21).

**Solomon's reading:** Magog appears as a son of Japheth (Gen 10:2), people of the far north. John universalizes the image: "the four corners of the earth." Rosh is more likely the Hebrew word for "chief" (as in the LSB) than Russia. Ezekiel's point, which John keeps, is that God alone wins the battle.

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** A coalition from the "four corners" networked against a defenseless, "living securely" people, and no human army wins. The defeat comes from heaven. Avoid mapping real modern nations onto the prophecy.

## The restrainer

**Type:** Unidentified (power or person)  
**Also called:** Ho katechōn / to katechon

**Key texts:** 2 Thessalonians 2:6-7

> "only he who now restrains will do so until he is taken out of the way" (2 Thessalonians 2:7, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** "And you know what restrains him now, so that in his time he will be revealed. For the mystery of lawlessness is already at work; only he who now restrains will do so until he is taken out of the way" (2 Thess 2:6-7). Paul uses both a neuter ("what restrains") and a masculine ("he who restrains").

**Role in the end times:** Holds back the full revelation of the man of lawlessness until God's appointed time.

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* The Holy Spirit working through the church, removed at the rapture (many dispensationalists). Others: human government.
- *Preterist:* The Roman Empire and Claudius (whose name echoes "restraint"), or the Jewish state.
- *Historicist:* The Roman Empire, whose fall allowed the papacy to rise (a classic Protestant reading).
- *Idealist:* God's providential restraint through law, government, and gospel.

**Solomon's reading:** The Thessalonians knew what Paul meant ("you know"). We do not, and humility is fitting here. Augustine admitted he did not know (City of God 20.19). Rule of law under God's providence, which fits Romans 13, is the most common historic view. Wherever you land, God ordains both the restraint and its timing.

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** The "restrainer" is a natural story device: the rule of law, a person, an institution, or a technical safeguard whose removal unleashes the lawless one. Keep the theology: the restraint is God's doing, and the lawless one is revealed "in his time."

## Daniel's four beasts

**Type:** Beast (kingdoms)

**Key texts:** Daniel 7:1-8; Daniel 7:17-27; Daniel 2:31-45; Revelation 13:1-2

> "four great beasts were coming up from the sea, different from one another" (Daniel 7:3, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** "Four great beasts were coming up from the sea, different from one another" (Dan 7:3). The first is a lion with eagle's wings, the second a bear raised on one side with three ribs in its mouth, the third a leopard with four wings and four heads, and the fourth "fearsome and terrifying and extraordinarily strong; and it had large iron teeth... and it had ten horns" (7:4-7). "These great beasts, which are four in number, are four kings who will arise from the earth" (7:17).

**Role in the end times:** Successive world empires, beastly human power, before the Son of Man receives the everlasting kingdom. They parallel the statue of Daniel 2 (gold, silver, bronze, iron and clay).

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* Babylon, Medo-Persia, Greece, Rome; the ten horns are a future revived Roman confederation.
- *Preterist:* The conservative identification (Babylon, Medo-Persia, Greece, Rome) is fulfilled in Christ's first coming. Critical scholars: Babylon, Media, Persia, Greece.
- *Historicist:* Babylon, Medo-Persia, Greece, Rome, with the little horn as the papacy.
- *Idealist:* Beastly kingdoms in every age contrasted with the humane kingdom of the Son of Man.

**Solomon's reading:** The historic church reading (Hippolytus, Jerome, the Reformers) is Babylon, Medo-Persia, Greece, Rome. It fits Daniel 8, which names Media-Persia and Greece (8:20-21). The theological point: human empires are beasts, and God's kingdom comes with a Son of Man.

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** Beasts rise "from the sea" when "the four winds of heaven were stirring up the great sea" (7:2). Chaos breeds empires. A novelist could set the rise of a fictional technocracy against Daniel's sea, but keep the end in view: the beast's dominion is taken away (7:12, 26).

## The little horn

**Type:** Human (prophetic)

**Key texts:** Daniel 7:8; Daniel 7:11; Daniel 7:20-26; Daniel 8:9-14; Daniel 8:23-25

> "a mouth speaking great boasts" (Daniel 7:8, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** In Daniel 7, "another horn, a little one" rises among the ten and uproots three, with "eyes like the eyes of a man and a mouth speaking great boasts" (7:8). "He will speak words against the Most High and wear down the saints of the Highest One, and he will intend to make changes in seasons and in law" for "a time, times, and half a time" (7:25). In Daniel 8, a different little horn grows from one of the goat's four horns, stops the regular sacrifice, and casts truth to the ground (8:9-12). He is "a king... insolent and skilled in intrigue" (8:23).

**Role in the end times:** The blasphemous persecutor of God's people before the kingdom is given to the saints.

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* The Daniel 7 horn is the future Antichrist from a revived Rome. The Daniel 8 horn is Antiochus IV and a type of the Antichrist.
- *Preterist:* The Daniel 7 horn is a Roman emperor (Nero or Vespasian, in some). The Daniel 8 horn is Antiochus IV.
- *Historicist:* The papacy (Daniel 7). Daniel 8 is often Antiochus or Islam.
- *Idealist:* The persecuting tyrant-type, with Antiochus as the pattern for the final enemy.

**Solomon's reading:** Daniel 8's horn as Antiochus IV Epiphanes (175-164 BC) is nearly certain from the history recorded in 1 Maccabees 1. Daniel 7's horn grows from the fourth kingdom and is judged when the Son of Man receives the kingdom, which is why the church has long linked it with the final Antichrist (2 Thess 2; Rev 13).

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** "Skilled in intrigue" (8:23) and intending "to make changes in seasons and in law" (7:25). A ruler who rewrites calendars and laws with the help of a machine. This kind of control, reshaping time and truth (it will "throw truth down to the ground," 8:12), fits the text.

## The ram and the goat

**Type:** Beast (kingdoms)

**Key texts:** Daniel 8:1-27

> "the shaggy goat is the king of Greece" (Daniel 8:21, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** A ram with two horns, ("one was longer than the other, with the longer one coming up last") butts westward, northward, and southward. Then "a male goat was coming from the west over the surface of the whole earth without touching the ground" with "a conspicuous horn between its eyes," shatters the ram, and its horn breaks into four (Dan 8:3-8). Gabriel explains: "The ram which you saw with the two horns is the kings of Media and Persia. Now the shaggy goat is the king of Greece, and the large horn that is between his eyes is the first king" (8:20-21).

**Role in the end times:** A rare prophecy with the interpretation given in the text: Medo-Persia, Greece (Alexander the Great), and the four successor kingdoms.

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* Fulfilled historically. The little horn that follows is a type of the Antichrist (8:17, "the time of the end").
- *Preterist:* Fulfilled historically in Alexander and his successors.
- *Historicist:* Fulfilled historically.
- *Idealist:* Fulfilled historically, with lessons about pride.

**Solomon's reading:** Everyone agrees on the identifications because the text names them. Alexander's lightning conquest (334-323 BC) and the fourfold split of his empire fit exactly. This is a model for reading prophecy: let Scripture interpret its own symbols first.

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** A goat that crosses the earth "without touching the ground" is an image of speed. A novelist could use it as an epigraph for a fast-moving tech conquest, while noting in an afterword that the text's referent is Alexander.

## The king of the North and the king of the South

**Type:** Human (kingdoms)

**Key texts:** Daniel 11:2-45; Daniel 12:1-3

> "he will honor a god of fortresses" (Daniel 11:38, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** Daniel 11 traces a long struggle between "the king of the South" and "the king of the North" (11:5-6 onward), matching in great detail the wars between the Ptolemies of Egypt and the Seleucids of Syria. It climaxes in a king who "will exalt and magnify himself above every god and will speak astonishing things against the God of gods" and honors "a god of fortresses" (11:36-38). Then comes "the time of the end" (11:40) and the great distress and resurrection (12:1-2).

**Role in the end times:** The historical backdrop to Antiochus IV and, in many readings, a prophetic picture of the final Antichrist.

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* 11:2-35 is fulfilled history (Ptolemies and Seleucids). 11:36-45 jumps to the future Antichrist.
- *Preterist:* All of 11:2-45 concerns Antiochus IV (or ends with Rome or Herod in some readings).
- *Historicist:* Applied to the papacy, Napoleon, the Ottomans, and others.
- *Idealist:* Antiochus as the type of every tyrant, with 11:36-45 transitioning to the final foe.

**Solomon's reading:** The detailed fit of 11:5-35 to Ptolemaic and Seleucid history is remarkable (Jerome documented it from Porphyry's own data in his Commentary on Daniel). Verses 36-45 do not match Antiochus's known end, and 12:2 jumps to the resurrection, so a typological leap to the final enemy (Jerome; many conservatives) is reasonable.

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** "He will honor a god of fortresses" (11:38). A tyrant whose real god is security and defense, served with "gold, silver, costly stones." A modern "god of fortresses" could be an AI defense system the ruler trusts above all.

## Elijah

**Type:** Human (prophet)

**Key texts:** Malachi 4:5-6; 2 Kings 2:11; Matthew 11:14; Matthew 17:3; Matthew 17:10-13; Revelation 11:5-6; Luke 1:17

> "I am going to send you Elijah the prophet" (Malachi 4:5, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** Elijah was taken up "by a whirlwind to heaven" without dying (2 Kgs 2:11). God promised, "Behold, I am going to send you Elijah the prophet before the coming of the great and awesome day of Yahweh" (Mal 4:5). John the Baptist came "in the spirit and power of Elijah" (Luke 1:17). Jesus said, "John himself is Elijah who was to come" (Matt 11:14). Elijah appeared with Moses at the transfiguration (Matt 17:3). One of the two witnesses has Elijah's power to shut the sky (Rev 11:6).

**Role in the end times:** The forerunner who prepares for the Day of the Lord by turning hearts.

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* John fulfilled it in part. A future Elijah (possibly one of the two witnesses) will come before the return.
- *Preterist:* Fulfilled in John the Baptist.
- *Historicist:* Fulfilled in John, and in reforming preachers.
- *Idealist:* Fulfilled in John, and continued in the prophetic witness of the church (Rev 11).

**Solomon's reading:** Jesus' own words identify John as the promised Elijah "if you are willing to accept it." Revelation 11 shows the Elijah-pattern continuing in end-time witness. A further personal return is possible but not required by the text.

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** An Elijah figure who stands against a regime's false fire (1 Kgs 18) is a powerful archetype for a story where the false prophet "makes fire come down out of heaven" (Rev 13:13). Do not claim the character is the literal Elijah unless the story clearly marks it as fiction.

## False christs and false prophets

**Type:** Human (deceivers)

**Key texts:** Matthew 24:4-5; Matthew 24:11; Matthew 24:23-27; Mark 13:22; 1 John 4:1-3; 2 Peter 2:1; Deuteronomy 13:1-5

> "false christs and false prophets will arise and will show great signs and wonders" (Matthew 24:24, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** "See to it that no one deceives you. For many will come in My name, saying, 'I am the Christ,' and will deceive many" (Matt 24:4-5). "False christs and false prophets will arise and will show great signs and wonders, so as to deceive, if possible, even the elect" (24:24). "If anyone says to you, 'Behold, here is the Christ,' or 'There He is,' do not believe him" (24:23).

**Role in the end times:** Recurring deceivers throughout the age, culminating in the false prophet. Deception is the dominant danger Jesus names in His end-times teaching.

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* They increase in the final period, ending with the Antichrist and false prophet.
- *Preterist:* Fulfilled in first-century messianic pretenders such as Theudas and the Egyptian (Acts 5:36; 21:38; Josephus).
- *Historicist:* Heretical movements across church history.
- *Idealist:* Constant across the age; "many false prophets have gone out into the world" (1 John 4:1).

**Solomon's reading:** Jesus' answer to "when?" begins with "see to it that no one deceives you." His test is simple: His real coming is unmistakable, "just as the lightning comes from the east" (24:27). Anyone who says Christ is secretly here is false.

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** This is the clearest AI theme in the Gospels. Generated "christs" with signs and wonders, livestreamed in the wilderness or "in the inner rooms" (24:26). Jesus' instruction to not go out and not believe gives your faithful characters a clear rule to live by. Wise Disciple's video "Joe Rogan Thinks AI Could Hide the Return of Jesus" argues exactly this from Acts 1:11 and 1 Cor 15:51-52.

## The martyrs under the altar

**Type:** Human (departed saints)

**Key texts:** Revelation 6:9-11; Revelation 20:4; Revelation 2:13; Revelation 12:11

> "How long, O Master, holy and true?" (Revelation 6:10, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** "I saw underneath the altar the souls of those who had been slain because of the word of God, and because of the witness which they had maintained; and they cried out with a loud voice, saying, 'How long, O Master, holy and true? Will You not judge and avenge our blood on those who dwell on the earth?'" Each is given a white robe and told to rest "for a little while longer" until the number of their fellow servants is complete (Rev 6:9-11).

**Role in the end times:** The witnesses who died faithful, alive with God now. Their prayer for justice drives the judgments (compare 8:3-5), and they reign with Christ (20:4).

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* Martyrs of the future tribulation (or all martyrs up to then).
- *Preterist:* Victims of Jewish and Neronian persecution.
- *Historicist:* Martyrs of the pagan Roman persecutions.
- *Idealist:* All faithful martyrs, a picture of the intermediate state.

**Solomon's reading:** They are "souls" (psychai): conscious, waiting, robed, and heard. This is one of Scripture's clearest windows into the intermediate state. Their prayer is answered in the judgments that follow.

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** Antipas, "My faithful one, who was killed" (2:13), is the only martyr Revelation names. A minor character who refuses the mark and is remembered by Christ by name could carry the moral weight of the book.

## The bride of the Lamb

**Type:** Human (corporate)  
**Also called:** The wife of the Lamb; the holy city

**Key texts:** Revelation 19:7-9; Revelation 21:2; Revelation 21:9-10; Revelation 22:17; Ephesians 5:25-27; Isaiah 54:5; Hosea 2:19-20

> "His bride has made herself ready" (Revelation 19:7, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** "The marriage of the Lamb has come and His bride has made herself ready." "It was given to her to clothe herself in fine linen, bright and clean; for the fine linen is the righteous acts of the saints" (Rev 19:7-8). The angel says, "I will show you the bride, the wife of the Lamb," and shows John "the holy city, Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven from God" (21:9-10). "The Spirit and the bride say, 'Come'" (22:17).

**Role in the end times:** The redeemed people of God united with Christ forever. She is the counterpart of the harlot.

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* The church, distinct from Israel in many dispensational systems.
- *Preterist:* The church, the new covenant people replacing the "harlot" of apostate Jerusalem (Gentry).
- *Historicist:* The true church.
- *Idealist:* All God's people in both covenants, glorified.

**Solomon's reading:** Two women, two cities: Babylon the harlot and Jerusalem the bride. Both are shown by a bowl angel (17:1; 21:9). The bride's clothing is "given" to her (grace) and consists of the saints' righteous acts (fruit).

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** A story can mirror harlot and bride: one adorned in purchased luxury and synthetic glamour, the other in clean linen "given" to her. The bride's role in the closing scene is to invite (22:17).

## Death and Hades

**Type:** Personified power

**Key texts:** Revelation 1:18; Revelation 6:8; Revelation 20:13-14; 1 Corinthians 15:26; 1 Corinthians 15:54-55; Isaiah 25:8; Hosea 13:14

> "I have the keys of death and of Hades" (Revelation 1:18, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** The rider of the pale horse "had the name Death, and Hades was following with him" (Rev 6:8). Christ holds "the keys of death and of Hades" (1:18). At the end, "death and Hades gave up the dead which were in them... Then death and Hades were thrown into the lake of fire. This is the second death" (20:13-14).

**Role in the end times:** The last enemies, personified. They serve as agents of judgment and are then destroyed. "The last enemy to be abolished is death" (1 Cor 15:26).

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* Personified powers whose destruction follows the millennium.
- *Preterist:* Personified powers, including in the first-century judgments.
- *Historicist:* Personified powers.
- *Idealist:* Personified powers in every age, abolished at the end.

**Solomon's reading:** Death is not a natural friend but an enemy that Christ defeated and will abolish. In the new creation, "there will no longer be any death" (21:4).

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** Transhumanist dreams of defeating death with technology contrast with the biblical hope. Only the One with the keys abolishes death, and He does it through resurrection, not uploading. A character tempted by digital immortality could meet the real promise of Revelation 21:4.

## The strong angel with the little scroll

**Type:** Angelic

**Key texts:** Revelation 10:1-11; Revelation 5:2; Ezekiel 2:8-3:3; Daniel 12:7

> "Seal up the things which the seven peals of thunder have spoken and do not write them" (Revelation 10:4, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** "Another strong angel coming down out of heaven, clothed with a cloud, and the rainbow was upon his head, and his face was like the sun, and his feet like pillars of fire" (Rev 10:1). He has "a little scroll which was open," stands on sea and land, and swears "that there will be delay no longer" (10:6). John is told to seal up the seven thunders and to eat the scroll: sweet in the mouth, bitter in the stomach (10:9-10).

**Role in the end times:** Recommissions John to prophesy again "about many peoples and nations and tongues and kings" (10:11).

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* A mighty angel (some say Christ) announcing the final phase.
- *Preterist:* The message of judgment on Jerusalem.
- *Historicist:* Often tied to the Reformation's recovery of the open Bible.
- *Idealist:* The church's bittersweet prophetic calling.

**Solomon's reading:** The open scroll recalls the scroll of chapter 5, now opened. The sealed thunders remind us that God has not told us everything (compare Deut 29:29).

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** Seven thunders that John was forbidden to write make an honest limit for any novelist. Do not "reveal" what God sealed. A story might let a character face the temptation to claim secret knowledge.

## The angel with the key of the abyss

**Type:** Angelic

**Key texts:** Revelation 20:1-3; Revelation 9:1; Matthew 12:29

> "having the key of the abyss and a great chain in his hand" (Revelation 20:1, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** "Then I saw an angel coming down from heaven, having the key of the abyss and a great chain in his hand. And he laid hold of the dragon, the serpent of old, who is the devil and Satan, and bound him for a thousand years" (Rev 20:1-2).

**Role in the end times:** Binds Satan, showing how easily heaven restrains him. A single unnamed angel does it.

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* A future binding after the return.
- *Preterist:* Christ's victory at the first coming.
- *Historicist:* Varied.
- *Idealist:* Christ's binding of the strong man (Matt 12:29), applied through the gospel age.

**Solomon's reading:** Satan is not God's equal. An unnamed angel with a chain is enough. This undercuts all dualism.

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** Whatever the scale of evil in your story, the climax of Revelation is anticlimactic for the villain: one angel, one chain.

## The angel with the eternal gospel

**Type:** Angelic

**Key texts:** Revelation 14:6-7; Matthew 24:14

> "Fear God, and give Him glory, because the hour of His judgment has come" (Revelation 14:7, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** "Another angel flying in midheaven, having an eternal gospel to proclaim to those who inhabit the earth, and to every nation and tribe and tongue and people. And he said with a loud voice, 'Fear God, and give Him glory, because the hour of His judgment has come; worship Him who made the heaven and the earth and sea and springs of waters'" (Rev 14:6-7).

**Role in the end times:** A last global call to worship the Creator. It is followed by angels announcing Babylon's fall and warning against the mark (14:8-11).

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* A literal angelic proclamation heard worldwide in the tribulation.
- *Preterist:* The gospel's spread before AD 70.
- *Historicist:* The modern missionary movement.
- *Idealist:* The church's ongoing proclamation in heaven's perspective.

**Solomon's reading:** Even in the darkest chapter, God calls every nation to worship the Creator. The choice in Revelation 13-14 is always worship: the beast or God.

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** A message reaching every language at once, "in midheaven" (overhead). A novelist could show it through a global broadcast that no system can censor, but keep it a call to worship God the Creator, not a technological trick.

## The kings and merchants of the earth

**Type:** Human (corporate)

**Key texts:** Revelation 6:15-17; Revelation 17:2; Revelation 18:3; Revelation 18:9-19; Revelation 19:19; Revelation 21:24; Psalm 2:1-3

> "the merchants of the earth have become rich by the power of her sensuality" (Revelation 18:3, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** The kings of the earth commit immorality with Babylon, and "the merchants of the earth have become rich by the power of her sensuality" (Rev 18:3). They mourn "standing at a distance" when she falls (18:9-17). The kings gather with the beast to war against the Rider (19:19). Yet in the New Jerusalem, "the kings of the earth will bring their glory into it" (21:24).

**Role in the end times:** The elites of the old order who profit from Babylon and resist the Lamb, and who appear, surprisingly, as redeemed nations bringing glory into the city.

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* Future rulers and global business leaders.
- *Preterist:* Client kings and merchants of the Roman world.
- *Historicist:* European powers.
- *Idealist:* The powerful in every age.

**Solomon's reading:** Revelation 21:24 is a note of hope: the nations and their kings are not simply destroyed but redeemed and bring their glory into God's city. That is the gospel's reach, not universalism (21:27).

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** A tech magnate who mourns "standing at a distance" as the system collapses, then is converted. Revelation 21:24 leaves room for that arc.

## The ten horns (ten kings)

**Type:** Human (kingdoms)

**Key texts:** Daniel 7:7; Daniel 7:20; Daniel 7:24; Revelation 13:1; Revelation 17:3; Revelation 17:12-17

> "they give their power and authority to the beast" (Revelation 17:13, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** "The ten horns which you saw are ten kings who have not yet received a kingdom, but they receive authority as kings with the beast for one hour. These have one purpose, and they give their power and authority to the beast" (Rev 17:12-13). They make war against the Lamb and lose, and they hate the harlot and burn her (17:14, 16).

**Role in the end times:** Allied rulers who hand their sovereignty to the beast, and then turn on Babylon.

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* A future ten-nation confederation (often a revived Roman Europe; some once pointed to the EEC).
- *Preterist:* Client kings or provincial governors of Rome.
- *Historicist:* The barbarian kingdoms that succeeded Rome.
- *Idealist:* The full number (ten) of earthly powers allied against God.

**Solomon's reading:** Ten is a symbolic round number in apocalyptic writing, and "one hour" marks their power as brief. Attempts to fix ten modern nations have repeatedly failed.

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** Ten partners who "give their power and authority to the beast," perhaps ceding control to a shared AI governance system, and later turn on the luxury city. The text's irony is that evil's coalitions devour themselves (17:16).

## The New Jerusalem

**Type:** Place

**Key texts:** Revelation 21:1-27; Revelation 22:1-5; Hebrews 11:10; Hebrews 12:22-24; Galatians 4:26; Isaiah 65:17-19; Ezekiel 48:35

> "the holy city, new Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven from God" (Revelation 21:2, LSB)

**What Scripture says:** "I saw the holy city, new Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven from God, made ready as a bride adorned for her husband" (Rev 21:2). It has the glory of God, twelve gates bearing the names of Israel's tribes, and twelve foundations bearing the apostles' names. It is a cube of 12,000 stadia, and John sees "no sanctuary in it, for the Lord God the Almighty and the Lamb are its sanctuary" (21:22). The river of the water of life and the tree of life are there, and "they will see His face" (22:4).

**Role in the end times:** The final home where heaven and earth are joined and God dwells with His people. Nothing "defiled" may enter (21:27).

**How the main approaches read it:**

- *Futurist:* A literal city, about 1,400 miles on each side (12,000 stadia).
- *Preterist:* Already present in the church (Heb 12:22), consummated at the end.
- *Historicist:* The church triumphant.
- *Idealist:* The glorified people of God pictured as a city-temple-garden. The cube recalls the Holy of Holies (1 Kgs 6:20).

**Solomon's reading:** The city is identified with the bride (21:9-10), so it is at least the people of God. The cube shows that the whole city is now the Most Holy Place. Whether the stones are also literal, the text's emphasis is God's presence: "Yahweh is there" (Ezek 48:35).

**For the novelist (fiction / speculation):** The last image of the Bible is a city, not a garden. Human culture is redeemed ("the kings of the earth will bring their glory into it," 21:24). A story about technology can end with hope that human making is purified rather than erased.
